Q: Which of the following statements concerning gap junctions is false? Gap junctions
*A. are exclusively found in cardiac muscle cells
B. electrically couple cells to facilitate and synchronize cellular communication
C. are composed of membrane proteins called connexions
D. allow ions and small molecules to diffuse from the cytosol of one cell to another cell
Explain: Gap junctions occur in many tissues (cardiac and smooth muscle, epithelia, etc.), so calling them exclusive to cardiac muscle is the false statement.

Q: Which of the following statements regarding extracellular chemical messengers is correct? Extracellular chemical messengers
A. bind exclusively to receptors located in the plasma membrane of target cells
B. generate a response in any body cell reached by that messenger
*C. can activate signal transduction pathways that culminate in a cellular response
D. directly bind to effector proteins within target cells to trigger responses such as muscle contraction or cell growth
Explain: Extracellular messengers act by triggering signal transduction pathways; they do not reach every cell, bind only membrane receptors, or bind effectors directly.

Q: Which of the following correctly describes an example of a hormonal signal?
A. Nitric oxide is released by endothelial cells lining blood vessels to trigger relaxation of neighboring smooth muscle cells.
B. Dopamine is released into the synaptic cleft by a presynaptic neuron.
C. The cytokine interleukin-2 is released by helper T immune cells to activate other nearby immune cells.
*D. The thyroid gland makes and releases chemical signals that circulate in the blood to regulate metabolism of target cells throughout the body.
Explain: Hormones are secreted into the bloodstream to act on distant target cells, unlike paracrine (NO), synaptic (dopamine), or local cytokine signaling.

Q: Which of the following statements about the transport of extracellular signaling molecules in the bloodstream is correct?
A. water-soluble hormones circulate in the bloodstream in “free” form lacking transport proteins
B. lipid-soluble hormones circulate in the blood temporarily attached to transport proteins
C. transport proteins make lipid-soluble hormones temporarily water-soluble, increasing their solubility in blood
*D. All of the statements are correct.
Explain: Water-soluble hormones travel free while lipid-soluble hormones bind carrier proteins that keep them soluble, so all three statements are true.

Q: Which of the following statements about chemical messenger-receptor signal interaction is FALSE?
A. Only a chemical messenger with a specific shape can bind to a given receptor.
*B. A given cell can serve as the target cell for only a single type of chemical messenger.
C. A given receptor may have different affinities for different chemical messengers with similar structures.
D. Chemical messengers so similar in structure that they can bind to the same receptor may compete with one another for that receptor.
Explain: A single cell typically expresses many receptor types and responds to numerous messengers, making that statement false.

Q: Which of the following statements about signal transduction pathways is FALSE? Signal transduction pathways
A. can differ in the number of relay proteins that they contain
*B. involve relay and effector proteins that are located exclusively in the plasma membrane of the target cell
C. are a specific sequence of events that occur between the binding of the extracellular messenger to the receptor and the cellular response
D. can involve the generation of a second messenger, for example, cyclic AMP (cAMP)
Explain: Relay and effector proteins can be cytosolic or nuclear, not confined to the plasma membrane, so that statement is false.

Q: The cellular response of a signaling pathway requiring transcription of a gene for an effector protein is _______ the response of a pathway where an effector protein must be phosphorylated.

C. unpredictable in duration compared to

Q: Which of the following extracellular chemical messengers activates a plasma membrane receptor?
A. the steroid hormone, aldosterone
B. the thyroid hormone, thyroxine (T4)
C. the gas, nitric oxide (NO)
*D. the peptide hormone, insulin

Q: The nitric oxide receptor contains

Q: Nodes of Ranvier are found in the

Q: The site of action potential generation known as the trigger zone is located in the axon hillock of
A. sensory neurons

Q: Both action potentials and graded potentials

*C. are deviations in membrane potential in a neuronal cell

Q: Which of the following ion channels are highly concentrated in neuronal axons?

*B. voltage-gated channel
C. mechanically-gated channel

Explain: Voltage-gated channels densely populate axons to generate and propagate action potentials.

Q: Given an extracellular Cl– concentration of 100 mM and an intracellular Cl– concentration of 10 mM, what is the equilibrium potential of a plasma membrane permeable only to Cl–?
*A. –61mV
B. –30.5 mV
C. 0 mV
D. +30.5 mV
E. +61 mV
Explain: For an anion, E = -61 x log([out]/[in]) = -61 x log(100/10) = -61 mV.

Q: Given an extracellular Na+ concentration of 150 mM and an intracellular Na+ concentration of 15 mM, what is the equilibrium potential of a plasma membrane permeable only to Na+?
A. –61mV
B. –30.5 mV
C. 0 mV
D. +30.5 mV
*E. +61 mV
Explain: For this cation, E = +61 x log([out]/[in]) = +61 x log(150/15) = +61 mV.

Q: Given an extracellular Cl– concentration of 100 mM and an intracellular Cl– concentration of 10 mM, what is the net direction of movement of Cl– across a membrane permeable only to Cl– when the membrane is at the equilibrium potential?

*B. there is no net direction of movement of Cl– across the plasma membrane
C. there is a net inward flow of Cl– across the plasma membrane
D. an initial net outward flow of Cl– is followed by a net inward flow of Cl–
Explain: At the equilibrium potential the electrical and chemical driving forces balance, so there is no net movement of the ion.
