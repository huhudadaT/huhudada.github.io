Q: Which statement about MHC molecules is TRUE? (Ignore any cross-presentation by dendritic cells.)
A. MHC Class I molecules present peptides to Helper T Cells.
B. MHC Class II molecules present peptides to Cytotoxic T Cells.
C. MHC Class II molecules are loaded with peptides from cytosolic proteins.
D. The diversity of MHC proteins is generated by genomic recombination.
*E. Options a) through d) are ALL FALSE.
Explain: MHC Class I presents to Cytotoxic T Cells and MHC Class II presents to Helper T Cells; diversity is generated by genomic recombination of receptor genes, not MHC genes — all listed options are false.

Q: The three phases of neuronal development are:
A. Nucleation, extension, and depolymerization.
*B. Genesis, outgrowth, and refinement.
C. Growth, branching, and arborization.
D. Adhesion, assembly, and retraction.
E. Birth, marriage, and death.
Explain: Neuronal development proceeds through genesis (birth of neurons), outgrowth (axon/dendrite extension), and refinement (pruning and strengthening of connections).

Explain: Hypermethylation silences gene expression and is a mechanism of tumor suppressor inactivation, not oncogene activation.

Q: Which of the following is TRUE regarding COPII vesicle formation?

Q: Which of the following is a difference between an action potential and a postsynaptic potential?

Q: If a cell has no Ran protein, what do you expect to occur?

Q: (CMC) Which experimental tool or approach allowed Randy Schekman to identify the Sec61 protein translocator?

*B. A yeast mutant cell line that changed the subcellular localization of an important enzyme in histidine biosynthesis.
C. The creative use of GTP-γS to enrich for vesicles containing Sec61.
D. A 'Move-It' experiment that moved Sec61 to a new subcellular location.
E. NONE of the options a) through d) contributed.
Explain: Schekman used yeast sec mutants — including those that mislocalized the histidine biosynthesis enzyme — to identify genes required for ER translocation and vesicle trafficking.

Q: (CMC) In the jackalope Hox gene scenario, which mutation could explain the absence of a ribcage (no rib extensions in thoracic segments T1–T3)?
A. Deletion of the HoxA gene.
B. Deletion of the HoxD gene.
C. Deletion of the HoxE gene.
D. Extension of HoxC gene expression anteriorly into segment FL.
*E. Extension of HoxD gene expression anteriorly into segment T1.
Explain: HoxD is normally expressed in lumbar segments (which lack ribs). By posterior dominance, extending HoxD expression anteriorly into thoracic segments T1–T3 imposes a lumbar-like (rib-less) identity on those segments, eliminating the ribcage.

Q: (CMC) Calcium activation of PKC and ERKII phosphorylation of K+ channels exemplifies which mechanism of maintaining plasticity-related changes over time?
A. Local protein synthesis.
B. Molecular switches.
C. Recurrent network dynamics.
*D. Bistable signaling networks.
E. Membrane trafficking.
Explain: The Ca²⁺/PKC/ERKII cascade with K+ channel modulation forms a bistable signaling network — the system can settle into either of two stable activity states, locking in plasticity-related changes long-term.

Q: (CMC) Which mechanism can reset misbehaving neurons for improved function in nervous system disorders?
A. Local protein synthesis.
B. Molecular switches.
*C. Recurrent network dynamics.
D. Bistable signaling networks.
E. Membrane trafficking.
Explain: Recurrent network dynamics allow the activity state of a network to be reset by altering input patterns, offering a therapeutic route to correcting pathological neural states.

Q: (CMC) If an E. coli infection occurs, which sequence BEST represents the order of immune events (earliest to latest)? I=T Cell-Independent B Cell Activation, II=T Cell-Dependent B Cell Activation, III=Dendritic Cell Activation, IV=Macrophage Activation, V=Somatic Hypermutation.
A. IV → III → II → V → I.
*B. IV → III → I → II → V.
C. IV → III → V → I → II.
D. II → V → IV → III → I.
E. V → IV → III → I → II.
Explain: Macrophages (IV) respond first, followed by dendritic cell activation (III); T Cell-independent B Cell activation (I) can occur quickly without T cells, while T Cell-dependent activation (II) requires Helper T Cell priming first, and somatic hypermutation (V) occurs later during affinity maturation.

Q: (CMC) For fluorescence imaging of cytoskeleton dynamics very close to the coverslip, which microscope is BEST suited?
A. Spinning disk confocal microscope.
B. Widefield fluorescence microscope.
C. Laser scanning confocal microscope.
*D. TIRF microscope.
Explain: Total Internal Reflection Fluorescence (TIRF) microscopy illuminates only ~100 nm above the coverslip, making it ideal for imaging events at or near the plasma membrane with minimal background.

Q: (CMC) For high-speed live imaging of fluorescently tagged receptor vesicular trafficking throughout the cell with the LEAST photobleaching, which method is BEST?
*A. Spinning disk confocal microscope.
B. Widefield fluorescence microscope.
C. Laser scanning confocal microscope.
D. TIRF microscope.
Explain: Spinning disk confocal microscopy acquires images rapidly using multiple simultaneous pinholes with low laser power, greatly reducing photobleaching compared to point-scanning confocal systems.

Q: (CMC) Which statement about lateral inhibition is FALSE?
A. At the beginning, all cells make both Notch and Delta proteins.
B. At the end, the specialized cell continues to make Delta protein, while the non-specialized cell stops making Delta protein.
C. Notch signaling prevents cell specialization.
D. The protein Delta that activates Notch is a membrane-bound ligand.
*E. Options a) through d) are ALL TRUE.
Explain: All four statements correctly describe lateral inhibition — cells start out expressing both Notch and Delta; the specialized cell ends up with high Delta while the non-specialized cell loses Delta; Notch signaling drives the non-specialized fate; and Delta is a membrane-bound ligand. Since none is false, the answer is "all are true."

Q: (CMC) To cause MORE cells in the morphogenetic field to become non-specialized, you would:
A. Remove Delta expression from the entire morphogenetic field.
B. Remove Notch expression from the entire morphogenetic field.
*C. Make all cells express Delta throughout lateral inhibition.
D. Make all cells express Notch throughout lateral inhibition.
E. BOTH options b) and c) would tilt the system toward non-specialized cells.
Explain: If every cell uniformly expresses Delta, then every cell's neighbors send strong Notch activation signals to it. Active Notch signaling drives the non-specialized fate, so uniform Delta expression locks all cells into the non-specialized state.

Q: (CMC) Enriched environments are associated with behavioral changes, but do not positively or negatively affect critical periods. This statement is:
A. ALWAYS TRUE.
B. SOMETIMES TRUE.
*C. ALWAYS FALSE.
Explain: Enriched environments DO affect critical periods — they can extend or reopen them in addition to producing behavioral changes — so the claim that they don't affect critical periods is always false.

Q: (CMC) The centers of the visual and auditory receptive fields can realign after prism-rearing. This statement is:
A. ALWAYS TRUE.
*B. SOMETIMES TRUE.
C. ALWAYS FALSE.
Explain: Realignment is possible during a critical period of plasticity, but the extent depends on the degree of prism displacement and whether the critical period is still open.

Q: (CMC) A protein with an N-terminal signal sequence AND an internal transmembrane domain — where does it end up?
A. In the cytosol.
B. In the extracellular space.
C. In the lumen of the ER.
D. In the nucleus.
*E. In the plasma membrane.
Explain: The N-terminal signal sequence directs cotranslational insertion into the ER; the transmembrane domain anchors the protein in the membrane, and it is ultimately delivered to the plasma membrane via the secretory pathway.

Q: (CMC) A protein with an internal transmembrane domain AND a nuclear localization sequence (NLS) — where does it end up?
A. In the cytosol.
B. In the extracellular space.
C. In the lumen of the ER.
D. In the nucleus.
*E. In the plasma membrane.
Explain: Without an N-terminal signal sequence, the protein is synthesized in the cytosol; the transmembrane domain inserts it into the ER membrane post-translationally if recognized, and it traffics to the plasma membrane; the NLS alone cannot direct a membrane protein to the nucleus.
