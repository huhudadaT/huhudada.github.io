Title: BIOSC 1250 Review 3 Short Set
Description: Condensed set of exam-style questions on the respiratory, urinary, and reproductive systems (Ch. 18, 19, 23).
Class: BIOSC 1250
Semester: Summer 2026

Q: Which alveolar cell type secretes surfactant?
A. Type I alveolar cells
B. Alveolar macrophages
*C. Type II alveolar cells
D. Goblet cells
Explain: Type II cells secrete surfactant to lower surface tension; Type I cells are the thin cells where gas exchange occurs.

Q: Compared with the systemic circuit, the pulmonary circuit is characterized by:
*A. High flow and low pressure
B. High flow and high pressure
C. Low flow and high pressure
D. Low flow and low pressure
Explain: The pulmonary circuit receives the entire cardiac output (high flow) but runs at low pressure (~25/8 mmHg).

Q: Sensory axons from the carotid bodies reach the brainstem via which cranial nerve?
A. Vagus (X)
B. Phrenic
C. Facial (VII)
*D. Glossopharyngeal (IX)
Explain: The carotid bodies are innervated by the glossopharyngeal nerve (IX); the aortic bodies use the vagus (X).

Q: The aortic bodies send their sensory information to the brainstem through the:
A. Glossopharyngeal nerve (IX)
*B. Vagus nerve (X)
C. Intercostal nerves
D. Hypoglossal nerve (XII)
Explain: Aortic body afferents run in the vagus (X); carotid body afferents run in the glossopharyngeal (IX).

Q: During inspiration the diaphragm contracts and thoracic volume increases. Alveolar pressure therefore:
A. Rises above atmospheric pressure
B. Stays equal to atmospheric pressure
*C. Falls below atmospheric pressure
D. Becomes equal to intrapleural pressure
Explain: By Boyle's law, increased volume lowers alveolar pressure below atmospheric, drawing air in.

Q: All of the following are true of cystic fibrosis EXCEPT:
*A. It primarily impairs cardiac muscle contraction
B. It results from a mutation in the CFTR gene
C. Chloride fails to exit the cell
D. It produces abnormally thick mucus
Explain: CF affects secretory epithelia (lung, pancreas, etc.) through defective Cl- transport; it is not a primary cardiac muscle disorder.

Q: A patient with emphysema has lost lung elastic recoil. This corresponds to:
A. Low compliance
B. High airway resistance
C. Increased alveolar surface tension
*D. High compliance
Explain: Emphysema raises compliance (easy to inflate, poor recoil); fibrosis instead lowers compliance.

Q: By the Law of LaPlace (P = 2T/r), why would small alveoli tend to empty into larger ones without surfactant?
A. A smaller radius produces a lower collapsing pressure
*B. A smaller radius produces a higher collapsing pressure
C. Surface tension is zero in small alveoli
D. Large alveoli have higher surface tension
Explain: Smaller radius means higher pressure, so small alveoli would empty into large ones; surfactant lowers surface tension most in small alveoli to offset this.

Q: Which lung volume cannot be measured directly by spirometry?
A. Tidal volume
B. Inspiratory reserve volume
*C. Residual volume
D. Expiratory reserve volume
Explain: Residual volume is the air left after maximal exhalation and cannot be exhaled, so spirometry cannot capture it.

Q: Oxygen is carried on hemoglobin bound to which component?
*A. The iron in heme
B. The globin amino chains
C. Plasma albumin
D. The 2,3-BPG binding site
Explain: O2 binds the Fe in heme, whereas CO2 binds the amino (globin) chains, a separate site.

Q: In metabolically active tissue, rising CO2 and H+ shift the oxygen-hemoglobin curve to the right. This indicates hemoglobin's affinity for O2:
A. Increases, loading more O2
B. Is unchanged
C. Increases only at high altitude
*D. Decreases, unloading more O2
Explain: The Bohr effect: greater acidity/CO2 lowers O2 affinity, promoting unloading where tissues need oxygen.

Q: Fetal hemoglobin has a higher O2 affinity than adult hemoglobin because it:
A. Binds more 2,3-BPG
*B. Binds less 2,3-BPG
C. Contains more iron atoms per molecule
D. Has a higher P50
Explain: BPG lowers O2 affinity; because fetal Hb binds BPG poorly, its affinity is higher, favoring O2 transfer across the placenta.

Q: The largest fraction of CO2 in blood is transported as:
A. CO2 dissolved in plasma
B. Carbaminohemoglobin
*C. Bicarbonate ions
D. CO2 bound to plasma albumin
Explain: About 70% of CO2 travels as bicarbonate (HCO3-); the rest is carbamino compounds and dissolved gas.

Q: The central chemoreceptors of the medullary respiratory center respond primarily to:
*A. A rise in CO2 and H+
B. A fall in arterial O2
C. A rise in arterial O2
D. A fall in blood pressure
Explain: Central chemoreceptors track CO2/H+; sensing low O2 is the role of the peripheral (carotid and aortic) chemoreceptors.

Q: The dorsal respiratory group drives the diaphragm through the phrenic nerve, which arises from which spinal levels?
A. C1-C2
B. T1-T4
C. L1-L3
*D. C3-C5
Explain: "C3, 4, 5 keep the diaphragm alive" - the phrenic nerve originates from C3 through C5.

Q: Carbon monoxide is dangerous largely because its affinity for hemoglobin is roughly:
A. Equal to that of O2
*B. 200 times that of O2
C. 20 times that of O2
D. One-tenth that of O2
Explain: CO binds hemoglobin about 200x more tightly than O2, blocking oxygen transport even at low concentrations.

Q: Which statement about gas exchange is NOT correct?
A. Dalton's law concerns the partial pressures of gases in a mixture
B. Henry's law concerns how much gas dissolves in a liquid
*C. During pulmonary gas exchange, O2 diffuses from the blood into the alveoli
D. CO2 is more soluble in plasma than O2
Explain: In pulmonary gas exchange O2 moves FROM the alveoli INTO the blood (and CO2 the reverse); the direction stated is backwards.

Q: Tubular secretion moves substances in which direction?
A. From the tubule into the blood
*B. From the blood into the tubule
C. From Bowman's capsule into the glomerulus
D. From the collecting duct into the renal artery
Explain: Secretion adds substances from blood into the tubular fluid; reabsorption is the opposite (tubule to blood).

Q: Blood enters the glomerulus through the afferent arteriole and exits through the:
A. Afferent arteriole
B. Renal vein directly
C. Vasa recta
*D. Efferent arteriole
Explain: The efferent arteriole carries blood away from the glomerulus - an unusual arteriole-to-arteriole arrangement.

Q: The Na+/K+/2Cl- (NKCC) cotransporter is located in which nephron segment?
A. Thin descending limb of the loop
*B. Thick ascending limb of the loop
C. Proximal convoluted tubule
D. Collecting duct
Explain: NKCC reabsorbs ions in the thick ascending limb, which is impermeable to water; the descending limb reabsorbs water, not ions.

Q: Regarding the loop of Henle, which statement is FALSE?
A. The descending limb is permeable to water
B. The ascending limb reabsorbs ions but not water
C. The descending limb does not reabsorb ions
*D. The ascending limb is freely permeable to water
Explain: The ascending limb is impermeable to water and reabsorbs ions only - "water falls" out of the descending limb.

Q: Principal cells of the collecting duct reabsorb ___ and secrete ___.
*A. Na+ ; K+
B. K+ ; Na+
C. H+ ; HCO3-
D. HCO3- ; H+
Explain: Principal cells reabsorb Na+ and secrete K+; intercalated cells handle the H+/HCO3- exchange.

Q: Intercalated cells help acidify the urine by:
A. Reabsorbing H+
B. Reabsorbing K+
*C. Secreting H+
D. Secreting HCO3-
Explain: Intercalated cells secrete H+ (and reabsorb HCO3-), which is why urine can be up to ~1000x more acidic than blood.

Q: The principal renal action of aldosterone is to:
A. Decrease Na+ reabsorption and increase K+ reabsorption
*B. Increase Na+ reabsorption and increase K+ secretion
C. Increase water reabsorption by inserting aquaporins
D. Increase the glomerular filtration rate
Explain: Aldosterone drives Na+ reabsorption and K+ secretion in the principal cells; water follows the Na+, raising blood volume and pressure.

Q: Antidiuretic hormone (ADH) concentrates the urine by:
A. Increasing Na+ reabsorption in the loop of Henle
B. Inhibiting renin release
*C. Inserting aquaporin-2 channels into principal cells
D. Dilating the afferent arteriole
Explain: ADH inserts aquaporin-2 into the principal-cell apical membrane, increasing facultative water reabsorption.

Q: Sympathetic stimulation of alpha-1 receptors on the renal arterioles produces:
*A. Vasoconstriction and a fall in GFR
B. Vasodilation and a rise in GFR
C. Renin release with no vascular effect
D. Increased aquaporin insertion
Explain: Alpha-1 activation constricts the arterioles and lowers GFR, while beta-1 activation triggers renin release.

Q: Which pressure is the primary force PROMOTING glomerular filtration?
A. Plasma colloid osmotic pressure
*B. Glomerular capillary hydrostatic pressure
C. Bowman's space hydrostatic pressure
D. Blood urea nitrogen
Explain: Glomerular capillary hydrostatic pressure pushes fluid into Bowman's capsule; the other two pressures oppose filtration.

Q: A substance that is freely filtered but neither reabsorbed nor secreted, so that its clearance equals GFR, is:
A. Urea
B. PAH
C. Glucose
*D. Inulin
Explain: Inulin (not insulin) is the classic GFR marker; PAH is secreted (clearance greater than GFR) and urea is partly reabsorbed (clearance less than GFR).

Q: If a substance has a renal clearance greater than its GFR, it must be:
A. Reabsorbed on net
*B. Secreted on net
C. Neither reabsorbed nor secreted
D. Not filtered at all
Explain: Clearance above GFR means the tubules add the substance to the filtrate (net secretion), as with PAH.

Q: Which structure acts as the countercurrent exchanger that preserves the medullary osmotic gradient?
A. Loop of Henle
B. Collecting duct
*C. Vasa recta
D. Glomerulus
Explain: The vasa recta passively exchange water and solutes to maintain the gradient built by the loop's countercurrent multiplier.

Q: The presence of which substance in the urine is considered NORMAL?
A. Glucose
B. Albumin
C. Ketone bodies
*D. Small amounts of urea
Explain: Urea is a normal urinary waste product; urinary glucose, albumin, or ketones each indicate a pathology.

Q: Crossing over between non-sister chromatids occurs during:
*A. Prophase I
B. Prophase II
C. Anaphase I
D. Metaphase II
Explain: Crossing over (tetrad formation and recombination) happens in prophase I of meiosis I.

Q: According to the textbook, homologous chromosome pairs and sister chromatids separate during which divisions, respectively?
A. Homologs in meiosis II; sister chromatids in meiosis I
B. Both separate in meiosis I
*C. Homologs in meiosis I; sister chromatids in meiosis II
D. Both separate in meiosis II
Explain: Textbook 23.1 states homologs separate in anaphase I and sister chromatids separate in meiosis II. (Your lecture slide 7 says meiosis II for the homologs, so confirm which version your professor grades.)

Q: Which testicular cells produce testosterone?
A. Sertoli cells
*B. Leydig (interstitial) cells
C. Spermatogonia
D. Primary spermatocytes
Explain: Leydig cells make testosterone; Sertoli cells support sperm, form the blood-testis barrier, and secrete inhibin.

Q: All of the following are functions of Sertoli cells EXCEPT:
A. Forming the blood-testis barrier
B. Secreting inhibin
C. Secreting androgen-binding protein
*D. Producing testosterone
Explain: Testosterone is produced by Leydig cells; Sertoli cells nourish developing sperm and form the barrier.

Q: Erection and ejaculation are mediated, respectively, by which autonomic divisions?
*A. Erection = parasympathetic; ejaculation = sympathetic
B. Erection = sympathetic; ejaculation = parasympathetic
C. Both parasympathetic
D. Both sympathetic
Explain: "Point and shoot" - parasympathetic drives erection (via nitric oxide), sympathetic drives ejaculation.

Q: Which accessory gland contributes the largest share (~60-70%) of semen volume?
A. Prostate gland
*B. Seminal vesicles
C. Bulbourethral glands
D. Epididymis
Explain: The seminal vesicles supply ~60-70% (fructose-rich, alkaline); the prostate adds ~25% and the bulbourethral glands ~5%.

Q: The prostate gland secretes a fluid that is:
A. Alkaline and rich in fructose
B. Alkaline and released before ejaculation to lubricate the urethra
*C. Slightly acidic, containing citric acid and clot-breaking enzymes
D. The main source of clotting fibrinogen
Explain: Prostatic fluid is milky and slightly acidic with citric acid and enzymes (e.g., PSA) that liquefy the semen clot; fructose and fibrinogen come from the seminal vesicles.

Q: In human oogenesis, the secondary oocyte is released at ovulation while arrested in:
A. Prophase I
B. Metaphase I
C. Anaphase II
*D. Metaphase II
Explain: The primary oocyte is arrested in prophase I until puberty; the secondary oocyte arrests in metaphase II and completes meiosis only if fertilized.

Q: How many functional gametes result from one primary oocyte completing meiosis?
*A. One
B. Two
C. Three
D. Four
Explain: Female meiosis yields a single ovum plus polar bodies that degenerate; male meiosis yields four sperm.

Q: From outermost to innermost, the layers of the uterine wall are:
A. Endometrium, myometrium, perimetrium
*B. Perimetrium, myometrium, endometrium
C. Myometrium, perimetrium, endometrium
D. Perimetrium, endometrium, myometrium
Explain: Perimetrium (outer serosa), myometrium (middle muscle), endometrium (inner lining).

Q: Just before ovulation, a sustained high level of estrogen exerts what kind of feedback on the anterior pituitary?
A. Negative feedback, suppressing LH
B. No feedback effect
*C. Positive feedback, triggering the LH surge
D. Positive feedback acting on the ovary only
Explain: Sustained high estrogen switches to positive feedback and triggers the LH surge; at moderate levels estrogen is inhibitory.

Q: The main role of human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) in early pregnancy is to:
A. Trigger ovulation
*B. Rescue the corpus luteum so it keeps producing progesterone
C. Initiate menstruation
D. Stimulate spermatogenesis
Explain: hCG from the early placenta maintains the corpus luteum until the placenta itself makes enough progesterone.

Q: In a genetic male, the Wolffian (mesonephric) ducts and Müllerian ducts do what, respectively?
*A. Wolffian ducts form the vas deferens; Müllerian ducts regress
B. Wolffian ducts form the fallopian tubes; Müllerian ducts regress
C. Wolffian ducts form the vas deferens; Müllerian ducts form the uterus
D. Wolffian ducts regress; Müllerian ducts form the vas deferens
Explain: Under testosterone the Wolffian ducts become male ducts (vas deferens), while the Müllerian ducts regress in males.

Q: Which gene on the Y chromosome initiates development of the testes?
A. DHT
B. hCG
*C. SRY
D. CFTR
Explain: The SRY gene directs the indifferent gonad to become a testis; without it, ovaries develop.

Q: Immediately after a sperm penetrates the oocyte, what blocks additional sperm from entering?
A. The corona radiata thickens
*B. The zona pellucida hardens (zona reaction)
C. The acrosome reseals
D. The oocyte completes meiosis I
Explain: Cortical granule release hardens the zona pellucida to block polyspermy.

Q: In what order does a sperm penetrate the coverings of the oocyte?
*A. Corona radiata, then zona pellucida, then plasma membrane
B. Zona pellucida, then corona radiata, then plasma membrane
C. Plasma membrane, then zona pellucida, then corona radiata
D. Corona radiata, then plasma membrane, then zona pellucida
Explain: The sperm crosses the outer corona radiata, then the zona pellucida, then fuses with the oocyte plasma membrane.
