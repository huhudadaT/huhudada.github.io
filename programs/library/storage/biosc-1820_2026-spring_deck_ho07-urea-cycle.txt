Name: BIOSC 1820 HO07 Urea Cycle
Description: Protein digestion, transamination, ammonia transport, and the urea cycle.
Class: BIOSC 1820
Semester: Spring 2026

Q: What hormone does protein in the stomach trigger?
A: Gastrin.

Q: What does gastrin trigger?
A: HCl and pepsinogen release.

Q: What hormone neutralizes pH in the small intestine?
A: Secretin (triggers bicarbonate release).

Q: What converts pepsinogen to pepsin?
A: Pepsin itself (autocatalytic).

Q: Where does pepsin cleave?
A: N-terminal of F, W, Y (very different from most proteases).

Q: Where does chymotrypsin cleave?
A: After F, W, Y.

Q: Where does trypsin cleave?
A: After K, R.

Q: Where are amino acids primarily oxidized?
A: In the liver.

Q: What enzyme catalyzes transamination?
A: Transaminase.

Q: What is the cofactor of transaminase?
A: Pyridoxal phosphate (PLP, vitamin B6).

Q: What residue holds PLP at the transaminase active site?
A: Lysine.

Q: What kind of kinetics does transaminase use?
A: Ping-pong (enzyme alternates between two states).

Q: What does glutamate + PLP produce?
A: α-ketoglutarate + PMP.

Q: What does oxaloacetate + PMP produce?
A: Aspartate + PLP.

Q: What is PMP?
A: PLP + NH3 (pyridoxamine phosphate).

Q: What enzyme releases free NH3 from glutamate?
A: Glutamate dehydrogenase.

Q: What molecule acts as an NH3 carrier to the liver?
A: Glutamate.

Q: What enzyme converts glutamate + NH3 → glutamine?
A: Glutamine synthetase.

Q: What enzyme converts glutamine → glutamate + NH3 in the liver?
A: Glutaminase.

Q: Besides amino acid catabolism, what else produces ammonia?
A: Nucleotide breakdown.

Q: What does glutamate + pyruvate produce?
A: Alanine + α-ketoglutarate.

Q: What carries NH3 from muscle to liver?
A: Alanine.

Q: What happens to alanine in the liver?
A: Alanine + α-ketoglutarate → pyruvate + glutamate.

Q: What is the glucose-alanine cycle?
A: Muscle Ala → liver → pyruvate → glucose → back to muscle → Ala.

Q: How does glutamate restore from α-ketoglutarate?
A: α-ketoglutarate + NH3 → glutamate.

Q: What is the nitrogen excretion product in aquatic animals?
A: NH4+ (ammonotelic).

Q: What is the nitrogen excretion product in vertebrates (and sharks)?
A: Urea (ureotelic).

Q: What is the nitrogen excretion product in birds and reptiles?
A: Uric acid (uricotelic).

Q: What enzyme makes carbamoyl phosphate?
A: Carbamoyl phosphate synthetase.

Q: What are the inputs to carbamoyl phosphate synthetase?
A: NH4+ + HCO3- + 2 ATP.

Q: What enzyme makes citrulline?
A: Ornithine transcarbamoylase.

Q: What are the inputs that make citrulline?
A: Carbamoyl phosphate + ornithine.

Q: What enzyme makes argininosuccinate?
A: Argininosuccinate synthetase.

Q: What is the intermediate before argininosuccinate?
A: Citrullyl-AMP.

Q: What two molecules combine to make argininosuccinate?
A: Citrullyl-AMP + aspartate.

Q: What enzyme cleaves argininosuccinate to arginine + fumarate?
A: Argininosuccinate lyase (argininosuccinase).

Q: Which urea cycle step is the only reversible one?
A: Argininosuccinate lyase.

Q: What enzyme makes urea?
A: Arginase.

Q: What are the products of arginase?
A: Urea + ornithine.

Q: What two factors regulate the urea cycle (broad)?
A: Ammonia mass action and N-acetylglutamate.

Q: What activates carbamoyl phosphate synthetase (the committed step)?
A: N-acetylglutamate.

Q: What is the structure of tetrahydrofolate?
A: Pterin – PABA – glutamate; CH2 bridge between N5 and N10.

Q: What is the degradation pathway of glycine in the kidney?
A: Glycine → glyoxylate → oxalate (via D-amino acid oxidase).

Q: What enzyme converts phenylalanine to tyrosine?
A: Phenylalanine hydroxylase.

Q: What disease results from phenylalanine hydroxylase defect?
A: PKU (phenylketonuria).

Q: What causes PKU symptoms?
A: Phenylalanine build-up and downstream products (phenylpyruvate, phenylacetate, phenyllactate).

Q: What is the glucose-alanine cycle's role?
A: Transfers nitrogen from muscle to liver while shuttling glucose back to muscle.

Q: Besides amino acid catabolism, what major source produces ammonia?
A: Nucleotide breakdown.

Q: Where in the cell does glutaminase work?
A: Liver mitochondria.

Q: Draw: glutamate
A: HOOC-CH(NH2)-CH2-CH2-COOH (5C amino acid with two carboxyls — alpha and gamma).

Q: Draw: α-ketoglutarate
A: HOOC-C(=O)-CH2-CH2-COOH (5C: carboxyl – ketone – CH2 – CH2 – carboxyl).

Q: Draw: oxaloacetate
A: HOOC-C(=O)-CH2-COOH (4C: carboxyl – ketone – CH2 – carboxyl).

Q: Draw: aspartate
A: HOOC-CH(NH2)-CH2-COOH (4C amino acid: amino acid backbone + CH2-COOH side chain).

Q: Draw: glutamine
A: H2N-C(=O)-CH2-CH2-CH(NH2)-COOH (glutamate with the gamma-carboxyl converted to an amide).

Q: Draw: pyruvate
A: CH3-C(=O)-COO- (3C: methyl – ketone – carboxylate).

Q: Draw: alanine
A: CH3-CH(NH2)-COOH (amino acid with methyl side chain).

Q: Draw: carbamoyl phosphate
A: H2N-C(=O)-O-PO3 (amide carbonyl linked through oxygen to phosphate).

Q: Draw: ornithine
A: H2N-CH2-CH2-CH2-CH(NH2)-COOH (5C amino acid; amino acid backbone with 3-CH2 side chain ending in -NH2; like lysine but one CH2 shorter).

Q: Draw: citrulline
A: Ornithine with the side-chain amino group replaced by ureido (-NH-C(=O)-NH2) on the delta carbon.

Q: Draw: citrullyl-AMP
A: Citrulline with AMP attached to the ureido oxygen (activated intermediate before argininosuccinate).

Q: Draw: argininosuccinate
A: Arginine's guanidinium nitrogen linked to aspartate's alpha-carbon (arginine + aspartate joined through -N-CH(COO-)-CH2-COO-).

Q: Draw: arginine
A: 6C amino acid: amino acid backbone + 3-CH2 side chain ending in a guanidinium group (-NH-C(=NH2+)-NH2).

Q: Draw: fumarate
A: HOOC-CH=CH-COOH (4C trans dicarboxylic acid with central C=C double bond).

Q: Draw: urea
A: H2N-C(=O)-NH2 (carbonyl flanked by two amino groups).

Q: Recognize: PLP (pyridoxal phosphate)
A: Pyridine ring with -CHO (aldehyde) at one position, -OH, -CH3, and a -CH2-O-PO3 phosphate group.

Q: Recognize: PMP (pyridoxamine phosphate)
A: PLP with the aldehyde (-CHO) replaced by an aminomethyl (-CH2-NH2) group.

Q: Recognize: uric acid
A: Purine-like fused 6+5 ring system with three C=O groups (a heavily oxidized purine).

Q: Recognize: pterin
A: Bicyclic ring system: a pyrazine ring fused to a pyrimidine ring (with -NH2 and =O on the pyrimidine).

Q: Recognize: tetrahydrofolate (THF)
A: Pterin (reduced) – PABA (para-aminobenzoate) – glutamate; the active 1-carbon unit binds at N5 and/or N10.

Q: Where on tetrahydrofolate is glutamate located?
A: On the right side.

Q: Where is the CH2 bridge in N5,N10-methylene-THF?
A: Between positions 5 and 10.
