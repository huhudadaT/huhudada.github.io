Title: Introduction to the Kidney and Renal Physiology
Description: Kidney functions, glomerular filtration and GFR, and the nephron from proximal tubule to collecting duct.
Kind: flashcards
Subject: BIOENG
Catalog: 1052
Semester: Fall 2026

Q: Who is considered the father of renal physiology?
A: Homer W. Smith.

Q: Which fish did Homer Smith travel to Africa in 1928 to study?
A: The lungfish.

Q: Why is the lungfish kidney useful for studying kidney evolution?
A: It resembles the kidneys of reptiles, birds, and mammals.

Q: What is the primary function of the kidney?
A: Filtering the blood and modifying it into urine.

Q: What are the three principal processes of the nephron?
A: Filtration, reabsorption, and secretion.

Q: What is the functional unit of the kidney?
A: The nephron.

Q: About how many nephrons are in a human kidney?
A: About 1 million.

Q: About how much plasma does a normal kidney filter per day?
A: About 180 L.

Q: What two regions is the kidney divided into?
A: The cortex and the medulla.

Q: Which kidney region is hypertonic relative to the other?
A: The medulla.

Q: What adaptation lets the kangaroo rat produce hyper-concentrated urine?
A: Extremely long loops of Henle.

Q: Which kidney hormone regulates blood pressure?
A: Renin.

Q: Which cells secrete renin?
A: Juxtaglomerular cells.

Q: Where are juxtaglomerular cells located?
A: At the vascular pole of the glomerulus.

Q: What triggers the RAAS cascade?
A: Decreased blood pressure.

Q: Which organ produces angiotensinogen?
A: The liver.

Q: What enzyme converts angiotensin I to angiotensin II?
A: ACE (angiotensin-converting enzyme).

Q: Where does ACE come from?
A: The lung and vascular endothelium.

Q: Which hormone does angiotensin II trigger from the adrenal cortex?
A: Aldosterone.

Q: What does aldosterone increase in the kidney?
A: Sodium and water reabsorption.

Q: Which kidney hormone stimulates red blood cell production?
A: Erythropoietin (EPO).

Q: What does the kidney sense to trigger EPO release?
A: Low oxygen delivery.

Q: What is the active form of vitamin D produced by the kidney?
A: Calcitriol.

Q: What cup-shaped structure encloses the glomerulus?
A: Bowman's capsule.

Q: What is the main driver of the glomerular filtration rate?
A: Glomerular capillary pressure (Pgc).

Q: What is the glomerular filtration rate?
A: The volume of glomerular filtrate formed per minute by the kidneys.

Q: What property do the glomeruli filter by?
A: Particle size.

Q: What are the three components of the glomerular filter?
A: Porous endothelium, glomerular basement membrane, and podocyte foot processes with slit membrane.

Q: Which cell contributes foot processes to the filtration barrier?
A: The podocyte.

Q: What is the most widely used biomarker of kidney function?
A: Creatinine.

Q: Which endogenous marker is more precise than creatinine?
A: Cystatin C.

Q: Name an injectable marker used to measure GFR.
A: Inulin.

Q: How is GFR calculated from a freely filtered marker?
A: (Urine concentration × urine flow rate) ÷ plasma concentration.

Q: Which equation is used to estimate GFR from blood?
A: The CKD-EPI creatinine equation.

Q: Which GFR method stays accurate in early kidney disease?
A: Measured GFR (mGFR).

Q: What is the filtration fraction equation?
A: GFR ÷ renal plasma flow.

Q: What is a typical filtration fraction?
A: About 20%.

Q: About how much ultrafiltrate do the kidneys produce per minute?
A: About 120 mL/min.

Q: What is the leading cause of kidney failure in the US?
A: Diabetes.

Q: What percentage of new kidney failure cases does diabetes account for?
A: 44%.

Q: Name a glomerular change caused by diabetic kidney disease.
A: Thickening of the glomerular basement membrane.

Q: What are the three broad stages of processing along the tubule?
A: Bulk reabsorption, fine tuning, and urine concentration.

Q: Which pump sets up the electrochemical gradient in every tubule segment?
A: The Na/K-ATPase.

Q: What fraction of total kidney mass is the proximal tubule?
A: About 65%.

Q: What luminal structure gives the proximal tubule its brush border?
A: Microvilli.

Q: Which transporter reabsorbs glucose in the proximal tubule?
A: SGLT2.

Q: Which proximal tubule transporter supports bicarbonate reabsorption?
A: NHE3.

Q: What does the proximal tubule secrete to adjust blood pH?
A: Ammonia (NH3).

Q: Which limb of the loop of Henle is permeable to water?
A: The descending limb.

Q: What osmolarity does the descending limb reach?
A: About 1200 mOsm/L.

Q: What happens to osmolarity along the ascending limb?
A: It falls from about 1200 to about 100 mOsm/L.

Q: Which loop of Henle transporter moves sodium, potassium, and chloride?
A: NKCC2.

Q: Which channel recycles potassium in the loop of Henle?
A: ROMK.

Q: Which transporter do loop diuretics inhibit?
A: NKCC2.

Q: What is the generic name of Lasix?
A: Furosemide.

Q: Which ions are selectively secreted in the distal convoluted tubule?
A: Potassium and hydrogen.

Q: Which DCT transporter moves sodium and chloride across the apical membrane?
A: NCC.

Q: Which DCT channel handles calcium entry?
A: TRPV5.

Q: Why are DCT cells rich in mitochondria?
A: They pump ions against their gradient.

Q: Which hormone controls aquaporins in the collecting duct?
A: Vasopressin (antidiuretic hormone).

Q: Which aquaporin sets water reabsorption in the collecting duct?
A: AQP2.

Q: Which channel reabsorbs sodium in the collecting duct?
A: ENaC.

Q: Where do the terminal collecting ducts empty?
A: At a papilla.

Q: How does black licorice raise blood pressure?
A: It increases ENaC abundance at the apical membrane.

Q: Of the 180 L of water filtered daily, how much is reabsorbed?
A: About 179 L.

Q: Which filtered substance is not reabsorbed at all?
A: Creatinine.
