Name: BIOSC 1820 HO11 Nitrogen-Containing Compounds
Description: Nitrogen cycling, heme synthesis, neurotransmitter derivatives, and nucleotide biosynthesis.
Class: BIOSC 1820
Semester: Spring 2026

Q: What is nitrogen fixation?
A: Conversion of N2 to NH3.

Q: What is nitrification?
A: Conversion of NH3 to NO2- to NO3-.

Q: What is denitrification?
A: Conversion of NO3- back to N2.

Q: What do organisms use NH3 for?
A: To make amino acids and other nitrogen-containing compounds.

Q: Is NH4+ oxidized or reduced?
A: Reduced.

Q: Are NO2- and NO3- oxidized or reduced?
A: Oxidized.

Q: What two molecules combine to start heme synthesis?
A: Succinyl CoA + glycine.

Q: What enzyme catalyzes the first step of heme synthesis?
A: Aminolevulinate synthase.

Q: What is the product of succinyl CoA + glycine?
A: Aminolevulinate.

Q: How many aminolevulinate molecules build one heme?
A: 8.

Q: What metal ion does heme contain?
A: Fe2+.

Q: What are many neurotransmitters and hormones derived from?
A: Modified amino acids.

Q: What enzyme converts tyrosine to dopa?
A: Tyrosine hydroxylase.

Q: What enzyme converts dopa to dopamine?
A: Aromatic amino acid decarboxylase.

Q: What enzyme converts dopamine to norepinephrine?
A: Dopamine hydroxylase.

Q: What is norepinephrine converted to next?
A: Epinephrine.

Q: What is the full catecholamine pathway from tyrosine?
A: Tyrosine → dopa → dopamine → norepinephrine → epinephrine.

Q: What enzyme converts glutamate to aminobutyrate (GABA)?
A: Glutamate decarboxylase.

Q: What enzyme converts histidine to histamine?
A: Histidine decarboxylase.

Q: What two enzymes convert tryptophan to serotonin?
A: Tryptophan hydroxylase, then aromatic amino acid decarboxylase.

Q: Why must cells constantly synthesize nucleotides?
A: The nucleotide pool is low (except ATP).

Q: What enzyme cleaves nucleic acids into oligonucleotides?
A: Endonuclease.

Q: What enzyme cleaves oligonucleotides into nucleoside monophosphates?
A: Phosphodiesterase.

Q: What enzyme converts nucleoside monophosphates to nucleosides?
A: Nucleotidases.

Q: What enzyme converts nucleosides to nucleobases?
A: Phosphorylases.

Q: What is a nucleoside?
A: A nucleotide without the phosphate (base + sugar).

Q: What is a nucleobase?
A: A nucleotide without the ribose and phosphate (just the base).

Q: What enzyme makes PRPP from ribose-5-phosphate + ATP?
A: PRPP synthetase.

Q: What is the product of ribose-5-phosphate + ATP?
A: PRPP + AMP.

Q: What enzyme uses PRPP + guanine to make GMP?
A: Phosphoribosyl transferase.

Q: What is the byproduct when PRPP + guanine → GMP?
A: PPi.

Q: How many steps are in purine biosynthesis?
A: 10 steps.

Q: What is the regulated enzyme of purine biosynthesis?
A: PRPP amidotransferase.

Q: What amino acids are required for purine synthesis?
A: 2 glutamine, 1 aspartate, and 1 glycine.

Q: What inhibits purine biosynthesis?
A: AMP and GMP (feedback inhibition).

Q: What is the first complete purine product of de novo synthesis?
A: IMP.

Q: What two purines is IMP converted to?
A: AMP and GMP.

Q: Draw: succinyl CoA
A: HOOC-CH2-CH2-C(=O)-S-CoA (4C dicarboxylate-like with one carboxyl and one thioester to CoA).

Q: Draw: glycine
A: H2N-CH2-COOH (smallest amino acid; no side chain, just H).

Q: Draw: aminolevulinate (5-aminolevulinate, ALA)
A: H2N-CH2-C(=O)-CH2-CH2-COOH (5C: amino group on C5, ketone at C4, carboxyl at C1).

Q: Draw: tyrosine
A: Amino acid with a para-hydroxyphenyl side chain (HO-C6H4-CH2-CH(NH2)-COOH).

Q: Draw: dopa (L-DOPA)
A: Tyrosine with an additional -OH on the ring (3,4-dihydroxyphenylalanine).

Q: Draw: dopamine
A: Dopa minus the carboxyl group: 3,4-dihydroxyphenyl-CH2-CH2-NH2.

Q: Draw: norepinephrine
A: Dopamine with an -OH on the beta carbon (3,4-dihydroxyphenyl-CH(OH)-CH2-NH2).

Q: Draw: epinephrine
A: Norepinephrine with N-methyl group (3,4-dihydroxyphenyl-CH(OH)-CH2-NH-CH3).

Q: Draw: glutamate
A: HOOC-CH(NH2)-CH2-CH2-COOH (5C amino acid with two carboxyls — alpha and gamma).

Q: Draw: aminobutyrate (GABA)
A: H2N-CH2-CH2-CH2-COOH (4C: amino group on C4, carboxyl on C1; glutamate minus the alpha-carboxyl).

Q: Draw: histidine
A: Amino acid with an imidazole ring side chain (-CH2-imidazole).

Q: Draw: histamine
A: Histidine minus the carboxyl: imidazole-CH2-CH2-NH2.

Q: Draw: tryptophan
A: Amino acid with an indole ring side chain (-CH2-indole).

Q: Draw: serotonin (5-HT)
A: Tryptophan minus the carboxyl, plus -OH at position 5 of the indole (5-hydroxytryptamine).

Q: Draw: ribose
A: 5-carbon aldose sugar; furanose ring with -OH on C1, C2, C3 and -CH2OH off C4.

Q: Draw: deoxyribose
A: Ribose with H instead of -OH on C2 (no 2'-OH).

Q: Draw: adenine
A: Purine base: fused 6+5 ring system with -NH2 at C6.

Q: Draw: thymine
A: Pyrimidine base: 6-membered ring with two C=O groups and a methyl group at C5.

Q: Draw: guanine
A: Purine base: fused 6+5 ring system with -NH2 at C2 and C=O at C6.

Q: Draw: cytosine
A: Pyrimidine base: 6-membered ring with one C=O (C2) and -NH2 at C4.

Q: Draw: uracil
A: Pyrimidine base: 6-membered ring with two C=O groups, no methyl (thymine without the methyl).

Q: Draw: PRPP
A: Ribose-5-phosphate with a pyrophosphate group on C1 (5-phosphoribosyl-1-pyrophosphate).

Q: Draw: IMP
A: Inosine monophosphate — hypoxanthine base attached to ribose-5-phosphate.

Q: Which bases are purines?
A: Adenine and guanine.

Q: Which bases are pyrimidines?
A: Cytosine, thymine, and uracil.
