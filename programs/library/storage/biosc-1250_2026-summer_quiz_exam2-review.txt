Q: Which of the following statements concerning muscle is true?
A. The three types of muscle tissue in the body include skeletal, cardiac, and striated.
B. Skeletal muscle works mainly in an involuntary manner.
C. The different types of muscle tissue are all controlled by somatic motor neurons.
D. Cardiac muscle works mainly in a voluntary manner.
*E. None of the statements concerning muscle are true.
Explain: 'Striated' is a description, not a muscle type; skeletal is voluntary; not all muscle is somatic-controlled; cardiac is involuntary — so none of the statements are true.

Q: Which of the following is a function of muscle?
A. producing body movements
B. stabilizing body positions
C. storing and moving substances within the body
D. generating heat
*E. All of these are functions of muscle.
Explain: Muscle produces movement, stabilizes posture, moves substances within the body, and generates heat — all are functions.

Q: Which of the following statements about skeletal muscle components is FALSE?
*A. A mature skeletal muscle fiber has a single nucleus located just beneath the sarcolemma.
B. Myofibrils extend throughout the sarcoplasm of a skeletal muscle fiber.
C. The sarcoplasmic reticulum stores calcium ions (Ca2+) in skeletal muscle fibers.
D. Transverse (T) tubules, invaginations of the sarcolemma, tunnel in from the surface toward the center of each muscle fiber.
E. Sarcomeres, composed of thin and thick filaments, are the repeating units of a myofibril.
Explain: FALSE question: mature skeletal muscle fibers are MULTInucleate, so 'a single nucleus' is the false statement.

Q: Contractile proteins in muscle include
A. actin
B. myosin
C. tropomyosin
*D. actin and myosin
E. actin, myosin, and tropomyosin
Explain: Actin and myosin are the contractile proteins; tropomyosin and troponin are regulatory proteins.

Q: Which of the following statements concerning muscle contraction is FALSE? During muscle contraction
A. thick and thin filaments slide past one another
B. Z discs come closer together
*C. the I band and A band narrow and eventually disappear when the muscle is maximally contracted
D. individual lengths of the thick and thin filaments remain unchanged
E. thin filaments are pulled toward the M line by the myosin heads
Explain: FALSE question: the A band width stays constant during contraction, so claiming the A band narrows/disappears is false. Anchor: A band = constant.

Q: A mutation in the gene encoding the dihydropyridine (DHP) receptor reduces the ability of the protein to sense depolarization events (i.e. the voltage sensitivity of the receptor is reduced). Patients with this mutant form of the DHP receptor
A. are paralyzed due to the inability of Ca2+ pump proteins to pump Ca2+ back into the sarcoplasmic reticulum
*B. are paralyzed due to an inability to release Ca2+ from the sarcoplasmic reticulum in response to an action potential
C. are paralyzed due to an inability of their skeletal muscle membranes to conduct action potentials
D. exhibit tremors due to the hyper-excitable plasma membranes of their skeletal muscles
Explain: The DHP receptor is the voltage sensor that triggers SR Ca²⁺ release; without it there is no Ca²⁺ release and the fiber is paralyzed.

Q: Which of the following statements regarding ATP production in skeletal muscle is true? In skeletal muscle
A. the ATP present is enough to power contraction for only a few seconds

Explain: ATP powers only a few seconds of contraction and is regenerated from creatine phosphate, anaerobic glycolysis, and aerobic respiration — all statements are correct.

Q: Which of the following statements concerning fatigue is correct?

C. The precise mechanisms that cause muscle fatigue are well understood.
*D. A number of factors contribute to muscle fatigue including the failure of motor neurons to release adequate acetylcholine.
Explain: Muscle fatigue is multifactorial and its precise mechanisms are not fully understood; peripheral factors include failure of excitation–contraction coupling.

Q: A motor unit consists of a

Q: Which of the following statements about a twitch is true? A twitch

Q: Which of the following skeletal muscle fiber types are MOST fatigue resistant?
*A. slow oxidative (SO) fibers
B. fast oxidative–glycolytic (FOG) fibers
C. fast glycolytic (FG) fibers

Q: Which of the following skeletal muscle fiber types generate the MOST powerful contractions?

B. fast oxidative–glycolytic (FOG) fibers
*C. fast glycolytic (FG) fibers

Q: Which of the following statements concerning intercalated discs is true? Intercalated discs

Q: Which of the following statements regarding smooth muscle is true? Smooth muscle
*A. is found mainly in the walls of hollow organs and tubes

C. contains both thick filaments and thin filaments arranged in orderly sarcomeres

Explain: Smooth muscle is found mainly in the walls of hollow organs and tubes; it is single-nucleate, lacks sarcomeres, and contracts as Ca²⁺ rises.

Q: Which of the following statements about the regeneration of skeletal muscle is true?
A. Mature skeletal muscle fibers retain the ability to undergo cell division.
B. Growth of skeletal muscle after birth is due mainly to hyperplasia, an increase in the number of fibers.
C. Satellite cells divide rapidly and fuse with existing fibers to fully replenish damaged skeletal muscle tissue.
*D. Growth of skeletal muscle after birth is due mainly to hypertrophy, the enlargement of existing cells.
Explain: Postnatal skeletal muscle growth is due mainly to hypertrophy (enlargement of existing fibers), since mature fibers cannot divide.

Q: After a skeletal muscle has contracted, which process is most important for lowering Ca++ levels in the cytoplasm?
A. closing of the Ca2+ release channels (ryanodine receptor)
B. activity of the acetylcholine receptor
C. closing of the dihydropyridine receptor
*D. activity of the Ca2+-ATPase pump
Explain: The SERCA (Ca²⁺-ATPase) pump actively transports Ca²⁺ back into the sarcoplasmic reticulum, lowering cytosolic Ca²⁺ so the fiber can relax.

Q: Release of calcium from the sarcoplasmic reticulum in smooth muscle cells can be due to activation of which types of channels?
A. IP3-gated channels and store-operated channels
B. IP3-gated channels and mechanically-gated channels
*C. Ca2+ induced Ca2+ channels and IP3-gated channels
D. Ca2+ induced Ca2+ channels and store-operated channels
Explain: In smooth muscle, SR Ca²⁺ release is triggered by IP₃-gated channels and by Ca²⁺-induced Ca²⁺ release (CICR).

Q: Which best describes a role of L-type Ca2+ channels in cardiac muscle cells?
A. They function exactly the same in cardiac muscle cells as they do in skeletal muscle.
B. They cause the absolute refractory period to be very brief.
C. They act as non-conducting voltage sensors that mediate excitation-contraction coupling.
D. They are directly responsible for making cardiac muscle fatigue-resistant.
*E. They are responsible for preventing tetanic contractions.
Explain: Cardiac L-type Ca²⁺ channels sustain the plateau, prolonging the refractory period so cardiac muscle cannot be tetanized.

Q: During an isotonic muscle contraction, what happens to the H zone?
A. It gets longer and shorter, successively.
B. It gets longer.
C. It does not change.
*D. It gets shorter.
Explain: Sliding filaments pull the Z discs inward, so the H zone (and I band) shorten while the A band width stays constant. Anchor: H and I shrink, A stays.

Q: The skeletal muscle action potential is MOST DIRECTLY important for which process?
A. activating the ryanodine receptor
B. triggering neurotransmitter release
C. promoting cross-bridge formation
*D. activating the DHP receptor
Explain: The muscle action potential travels down the T-tubule and most directly activates the DHP receptor (voltage sensor), which then opens the SR ryanodine receptor.

Q: Just like a neuronal action potential, a muscle action potential consists of a depolarizing phase caused by opening of voltage-gated sodium channels followed by a repolarizing phase due to opening of voltage-gated potassium channels and closing of voltage-gated sodium channels.
*A. True
B. False
Explain: A skeletal muscle action potential has depolarizing and repolarizing phases driven by voltage-gated Na⁺ and K⁺ channels, just like a neuronal action potential.

Q: In cardiac muscle cells, the release of Ca2+ through L-type Ca2+ channels depolarizes the sarcoplasmic reticulum and inhibits the release of any additional Ca2+ necessary for excitation-contraction coupling.
A. True
*B. False
Explain: FALSE: L-type channels admit only a small trigger Ca²⁺; most Ca²⁺ for cardiac contraction comes from the SR via CICR, so they do not 'directly' cause contraction.

Q: In skeletal muscle, which of the following is not found in a thin myofilament?

D. F actin
*E. Titin
Explain: NOT question: the thin filament contains F-actin, troponin, and tropomyosin; titin is a separate elastic filament, not part of the thin filament.

Q: Which incorrectly describes what happens during skeletal muscle contraction?

*B. A band

Q: Where in a sarcomere do actin and myosin overlap?

C. The I band
D. The Z disc
*E. The A band
Explain: Actin (thin) and myosin (thick) overlap within the A band.

C. have antigen B.
D. have antigen A and antigen B.
*E. produce both anti-A and anti-B antibodies.
Explain: Type O has neither A nor B antigens but produces both anti-A and anti-B antibodies — it is the universal DONOR, not universal recipient.

Q: Which of the following events occurs if a person with type B blood receives a transfusion of type A blood?
A. Donor anti-B antibodies bind to recipient anti-A antibodies and cause erythrocyte agglutination and hemolysis.
B. The donor’s anti-A antibodies become diluted in the recipient’s plasma.
C. Recipient anti-B antibodies bind to the B antigens on the donor’s erythrocytes causing erythrocyte agglutination and hemolysis.
*D. Recipient anti-A antibodies bind to the A antigens on the donor’s erythrocytes causing erythrocyte agglutination and hemolysis.
Explain: A type-B recipient carries anti-A antibodies, which attack the A antigens on transfused type-A cells and cause agglutination.

Q: Which of the following statements about the Rh blood group is FALSE?
A. People whose erythrocytes have Rh antigens are designated Rh+.
*B. An Rh− mother cannot have an Rh+fetus.
C. Hemolytic disease of the newborn occurs when maternal anti-Rh antibodies are passed through the placenta to an Rh+fetus.
D. People whose erythrocytes lack Rh antigens are designated Rh–.
Explain: FALSE question: an Rh⁻ mother CAN carry an Rh⁺ fetus (if the father is Rh⁺) — that mismatch is exactly what creates HDN risk.

Q: How do practitioners prevent hemolytic disease of the newborn during the second Rh+ pregnancy for an Rh– mom?
A. by giving a transfusion of Rh+ blood
B. by giving anti-D immunoglobulin (RhoGAM) to the newborn
C. by administering fetal hemoglobin intravenously
*D. by administering anti-D immunoglobulin (RhoGAM) to the mom.
E. by administering an intramuscular injection of heparin
Explain: Anti-D immunoglobulin (RhoGAM) is given to the Rh⁻ mother to stop her from making anti-Rh antibodies. Anchor: RhoGAM → MOM, not the newborn.

Q: The cardiac cycle proceeds in 5 phases. Which two phases make up the state "atrial diastole, ventricular systole"?
A. passive ventricular filling and atrial contraction
B. atrial contraction and isovolumetric ventricular contraction
*C. isovolumetric ventricular contraction and ventricular ejection
D. ventricular ejection and isovolumetric ventricular relaxation
Explain: While the ventricles contract and the atria are relaxed, the heart passes through isovolumetric ventricular contraction and then ventricular ejection. (Original was "select all that apply" → C and D.)

Q: The cardiac cycle proceeds in 5 phases. Which two phases make up the state "atrial diastole, ventricular diastole"?
A. atrial contraction and passive ventricular filling
*B. passive ventricular filling and isovolumetric ventricular relaxation
C. isovolumetric ventricular contraction and ventricular ejection
D. ventricular ejection and isovolumetric ventricular relaxation
Explain: With both chambers relaxed, the heart is in isovolumetric ventricular relaxation and passive ventricular filling. (Original was "select all that apply" → A and E.)

Q: Select the appropriate relationship between the valves opening/closing and pressure changes during the cardiac cycle.
A. Atrioventricular valves begin to close when the pressure in the aorta or pulmonary artery exceeds the pressure in the ventricles.
B. Semilunar valves begin to open when the pressure in the ventricles exceeds systolic pressure in the aorta or pulmonary artery.
*C. Semilunar valves begin to open when the pressure in the ventricles exceeds diastolic blood pressure in the aorta or pulmonary artery.
D. Atrioventricular valves begin to open when the pressure in the ventricles exceeds the pressure in the atria.
Explain: The semilunar valves open once ventricular pressure rises just above the arterial diastolic pressure; "systolic" (B) is too high, and the AV valves open when atrial pressure exceeds ventricular (D is reversed).

Q: Which statement is not true?
*A. During ventricular systole, the bicuspid valve is open.
B. During ventricular systole, the pulmonary semilunar valve is open.
C. During ventricular systole, the tricuspid valve is closed.
D. During ventricular systole, the aortic semilunar valve is open.
E. During ventricular systole, the atria are filling with blood.
Explain: NOT question: the bicuspid (mitral) valve is an AV valve and is closed during ventricular systole, so "the bicuspid valve is open" is the false statement.

Q: The Frank-Starling mechanism of the heart states that an increased venous return will normally result in a higher heart rate.
A. True
*B. False
Explain: False: the Frank-Starling mechanism links greater venous return (preload) to a stronger contraction and larger stroke volume, not to a higher heart rate.

Q: Agents such as calcium channel blockers increase the strength of contraction of the heart muscle and thus have a positive inotropic effect.
A. True
*B. False
Explain: False: calcium channel blockers reduce Ca²⁺ entry and weaken contraction — they are negative inotropes.

Q: The hydrostatic pressure difference between the plasma inside the capillary beds and the interstitial fluid surrounding the cells favors filtration (not reabsorption) of fluid.
*A. True
B. False
Explain: True: capillary hydrostatic pressure pushes fluid out of the capillary (filtration), most strongly at the arterial end.

Q: Which of the following is NOT primarily a function of blood plasma?
A. Transport of hormones
B. Having plasma proteins that exert an osmotic pressure favoring fluid absorption into the capillaries
C. Providing clotting factors that are ready to be activated
*D. Transport of oxygen
E. Being in osmotic balance with red blood cells
Explain: NOT question: oxygen is carried mainly by hemoglobin inside red blood cells, so oxygen transport is not primarily a plasma function.

Q: The smooth muscle in arteriole walls can be stimulated either to contract or to relax, thereby either decreasing or increasing the amount of blood flowing through arteriolar beds.
*A. True
B. False
Explain: True: arteriolar vasoconstriction reduces flow through a bed while vasodilation increases it.

Q: Hypertension is often treated with drugs that increase total peripheral resistance.
A. True
*B. False
Explain: False: antihypertensive drugs lower total peripheral resistance (e.g., vasodilators), which lowers blood pressure.

Q: Identify which of these does not apply to the myocardium.
A. has extensive network of blood vessels
B. made of cells that have many mitochondria
*C. is a fibrous membrane that stabilizes the heart
D. is a layer that contains cells connected by gap junctions
E. is the excitable thick layer between the epicardium and endocardium
Explain: NOT question: the fibrous stabilizing membrane is the fibrous pericardium, not the myocardium — the myocardium is the thick, vascular, gap-junction-coupled muscle layer.

Q: In pericarditis, excessive fluid accumulates in the pericardial cavity, impairing the heart's pumping ability. A needle to drain the fluid would be inserted through which of these?
A. myocardium
*B. parietal layer of pericardium
C. endocardium
D. visceral layer of pericardium
Explain: The fluid sits in the pericardial cavity between the visceral and parietal serous layers; the drainage needle enters from outside through the parietal pericardium.

Q: Heart valves ensure one-way blood flow. Which accurately describes the role of pressure differences in valve function?
A. AV valves open when pressure in the ventricles exceeds pressure in the atria.
*B. SL valves open when pressure in the ventricles exceeds pressure in the arteries.
C. SL valves open when pressure in the ventricles exceeds pressure in the atria.
D. AV valves open when pressure in the ventricles exceeds pressure in the arteries.
Explain: Semilunar valves open when ventricular pressure exceeds arterial pressure; AV valves open when atrial pressure exceeds ventricular pressure (option A is reversed).

Q: Cardiac muscle fibers differ from skeletal muscle fibers in that cardiac fibers are
*A. electrically coupled to one another
B. lacking cell junctions called desmosomes
C. multinucleated
D. longer and greater in diameter
Explain: Cardiac fibers are electrically coupled through gap junctions in their intercalated discs; they are uninucleate, shorter and narrower than skeletal fibers, and they do contain desmosomes.

Q: Which autorhythmic fibers start the impulse responsible for the P wave in the ECG?
A. Purkinje fibers
*B. SA node
C. AV bundle
D. AV node
Explain: The SA node fires the impulse that spreads across the atria, producing the P wave (atrial depolarization).

Q: Which channel event is responsible for the depolarization phase of the action potential in contractile cardiac muscle fibers?
A. closing of slow voltage-gated K+ channels
B. closing of voltage-gated Na+ channels
C. opening of fast voltage-gated K+ channels
*D. opening of voltage-gated Na+ channels
Explain: Rapid depolarization of contractile cardiac fibers is driven by the opening of fast voltage-gated Na⁺ channels.

Q: On an electrocardiogram, which wave represents repolarization of the ventricular muscle cells?
A. QRS complex
B. R wave
C. P wave
*D. T wave
Explain: The T wave represents ventricular repolarization. Anchor: QRS = ventricular depolarization, T = ventricular repolarization.

Q: ACh secreted from the parasympathetic vagus nerve endings in the heart causes
A. lower amplitude of action potential in the SA node
B. an increase in the duration of the action potential in the SA node
C. conduction to speed up between atria and ventricles
*D. hyperpolarization of cells in the SA node
Explain: Vagal ACh opens K⁺ channels and hyperpolarizes SA node cells, slowing the heart rate.

Q: What type of receptor mediates the positive inotropic effect of epinephrine or norepinephrine on heart contractility?
A. receptor tyrosine kinase
B. intracellular receptor
C. ion channel receptor
*D. G protein-coupled receptor
Explain: Epinephrine and norepinephrine act on β₁-adrenergic receptors, which are G protein-coupled receptors, to raise contractility.

Q: A patient's blood work shows decreased resistance to blood flow. Considering viscosity, which condition is most likely?
*A. anemia
B. dehydration
C. malaria
D. polycythemia
E. elevated albumin in plasma (hyperalbuminemia)
Explain: Anemia lowers hematocrit and therefore blood viscosity, which decreases resistance; dehydration, polycythemia, and hyperalbuminemia all raise viscosity and resistance.
