Q: Which of the following statements about morphogens or cellular responses to morphogens is FALSE?
A. Morphogens establish a gradient of inducer activity.
B. Cellular responses differ depending on levels of inducer activity.
*C. The response to morphogens is an example of autonomous development.
D. Morphogens play an important role in anterior-posterior patterning in Drosophila.
E. Rate of morphogen synthesis, diffusion, and removal all contribute to patterning.
Explain: Morphogen-dependent patterning is conditional (non-autonomous) development — cells read positional information from an external gradient rather than following a cell-autonomous program.

Q: Which term describes the formation of new vessels from EXISTING vessels?
*A. Angiogenesis.
B. Vasculogenesis.
C. Arteriogenesis.
D. Capillarogenesis.
Explain: Angiogenesis is the sprouting of new blood vessels from pre-existing ones, distinct from vasculogenesis which forms vessels de novo from progenitor cells.

Q: Which one of the following statements about birth order in neuronal development is FALSE?
*A. Birth order controls the cell identity of neurons from the head to the tail in the spine.
B. Birth order regulates identity of daughter cells from a neuroblast that stays on the neural tube lumen.
C. Birth order is responsible for creating layers of neurons in the brain.
D. Birth order of a neuron regulates how far it will migrate.
E. The statements a) through d) are ALL FALSE.
Explain: Birth order governs the layered identity of neurons within a single region (e.g., cortical layers), not the positional identity of neurons along the head-to-tail spinal axis, which is controlled by Hox genes.

Q: Which of the following protein families is NOT required to successfully form a vesicle?
A. Initiator proteins.
B. Coat proteins.
C. Adapter proteins.

*E. t-SNAREs.
Explain: t-SNAREs reside on the target membrane and are required for vesicle fusion, not for vesicle budding/formation; initiator proteins, coat proteins, adapter proteins, and cargo all participate in vesicle formation.

Q: Which of the following statements regarding immunofluorescent microscopy is FALSE?

Q: According to Hebb's Postulate, connectivity between A and B will be strengthened by which of the following? I. A is able to excite B. II. A and B are both excited by C. III. A repeatedly takes part in the firing of B with C.

Explain: Hebb's rule requires that cell A contributes to the firing of cell B; option II (both excited by C) does not require A to drive B's firing, so it does not meet the Hebbian criterion.

A. Fibronectin.
B. Collagen.
C. Laminin.
D. Proteoglycan.
*E. Glycosaminoglycans.
Explain: Glycosaminoglycans (GAGs) are highly negatively charged and attract large amounts of water, forming a hydrogel that enables cartilage and other ECM to resist compressive forces.

Q: (CMC) Which ECM component forms fibrils only when cells exert tension and open a cryptic site for polymerization?
*A. Fibronectin.
B. Collagen.
C. Laminin.
D. Proteoglycan.
E. Glycosaminoglycans.
Explain: Fibronectin is normally a soluble dimer; cell-generated cytoskeletal tension through integrin binding unfolds fibronectin, exposing cryptic self-assembly sites that allow fibril formation.

Q: (CMC) Viral peptides presented by infected host cells — which component of the immune system most likely interacts?
A. Antibodies.
B. Helper T Cells.
*C. Cytotoxic T Cells.
D. Helper T Cells and antibodies.
E. Complement proteins.
Explain: Infected host cells present endogenous viral peptides on MHC Class I molecules, which are specifically recognized by Cytotoxic T Cells (CD8+) for targeted killing.

Q: (CMC) Sugar molecules on the outside of extracellular bacterial pathogens — which immune component most likely interacts?
*A. Antibodies.
B. Helper T Cells.
C. Cytotoxic T Cells.
D. Helper T Cells and antibodies.
E. Cytotoxic T Cells and antibodies.
Explain: Carbohydrate/sugar antigens cannot be presented by MHC molecules and thus cannot activate T Cells; they are recognized directly by antibodies (T Cell-independent antigens).

Q: (CMC) Proteins on the outside of extracellular bacterial pathogens — which immune component most likely interacts?
A. Antibodies.
B. Helper T Cells.
C. Cytotoxic T Cells.
*D. Helper T Cells and antibodies.
E. Cytotoxic T Cells and antibodies.
Explain: Surface proteins of extracellular bacteria can be recognized by antibodies directly and, after phagocytosis and processing, presented on MHC Class II to Helper T Cells.

Q: (CMC) A protein with an N-terminal signal sequence but no other signal sequences — where does it end up?
A. As soluble protein in the cytosol.
B. As transmembrane protein in the plasma membrane.
*C. As soluble protein in the extracellular space.
D. As soluble protein in the lumen of the ER.
E. As transmembrane protein in the ER membrane.
Explain: An N-terminal signal sequence directs cotranslational insertion into the ER lumen; without a transmembrane domain or retention signal, the protein is secreted into the extracellular space.

Q: (CMC) A protein with a single internal hydrophobic domain but NO N-terminal signal sequence — where does it end up?
A. As soluble protein in the cytosol.
*B. As transmembrane protein in the plasma membrane.
C. As soluble protein in the extracellular space.
D. As soluble protein in the lumen of the ER.
E. As transmembrane protein in the ER membrane.
Explain: An internal hydrophobic domain can function as a signal-anchor sequence — it is recognized by SRP, directs the protein to the ER membrane, and is inserted as a transmembrane segment. The protein then trafficks through the secretory pathway and is delivered to the plasma membrane.

Q: (CMC) A specific location within the retinotopic coordinate system maps to a specific interaural time differential. This statement is:
*A. ALWAYS TRUE.
B. SOMETIMES TRUE.
C. ALWAYS FALSE.
Explain: The alignment between the visual retinotopic map and the auditory map (interaural time differences) is a fixed anatomical feature in the superior colliculus, making this always true.

Q: (CMC) The centers of the visual and auditory receptive fields can realign after prism-rearing. This statement is:
A. ALWAYS TRUE.
*B. SOMETIMES TRUE.
C. ALWAYS FALSE.
Explain: Realignment is possible during a critical period of auditory-visual map plasticity, but it depends on the degree of prism displacement and the age/developmental window of the animal.

Q: (CMC) Which of the following is TRUE of BOTH AMPA and NMDA receptors?
A. TRUE of AMPA receptors only.
B. TRUE of NMDA receptors only.
*C. TRUE of BOTH AMPA and NMDA receptors.
D. FALSE for BOTH AMPA and NMDA receptors.
Explain: Both AMPA and NMDA receptors are ionotropic glutamate receptors that open upon neurotransmitter (glutamate) binding, though they differ in their kinetics, ion permeability, and voltage-dependence.

Q: (CMC) Which of the following is TRUE about AMPA and NMDA receptor recruitment to the synapse in long-term depression?
A. TRUE of AMPA receptors only.
B. TRUE of NMDA receptors only.
C. TRUE of BOTH AMPA and NMDA receptors.
*D. FALSE for BOTH AMPA and NMDA receptors.
Explain: In LTD, AMPA receptors are removed from the synapse (internalized), not recruited; NMDA receptors remain relatively stable at the synapse and are not specifically recruited during LTD.

Q: (CMC) You introduce a temperature-sensitive vRas (stable at 37°C, degraded at 42°C) into normal fibroblasts and perform a soft-agar assay. Which statement is TRUE?
A. At 37°C, fibroblasts will transform and maintain their transformed phenotype at 42°C.
B. At 37°C, fibroblasts will transform, but lose their transformed phenotype permanently when raised to 42°C.
*C. At 37°C, fibroblasts will transform, lose their transformed phenotype at 42°C, but regain it when the temperature is lowered to 37°C again.
D. Transformation ability is not affected by temperature at all.
Explain: Oncogenic Ras drives transformation only while it is present; when temperature is raised, TS-vRas is degraded and transformation is lost, but it is restored when the temperature is lowered and TS-vRas is re-expressed.

Q: (CMC) In lateral inhibition, if a mutant Notch that can NEVER be activated (even when bound by Delta) is expressed in a single cell in the middle of the morphogenetic field, what outcome do you expect?
*A. The cell expressing mutant Notch will become Type A, but all neighboring cells will become Type B.
B. The cell expressing mutant Notch will become Type A and all neighboring cells will also become Type A.
C. The cell expressing mutant Notch will become Type B, but all its neighboring cells will become Type A.
D. The cell expressing mutant Notch will become Type B and all its neighboring cells will also become Type B.
E. Expressing the mutant Notch will NOT alter the ratio or spacing of Type A to Type B cells.
Explain: A cell unable to activate Notch cannot be inhibited by neighboring Delta signals; it will spontaneously specialize (become Type A) and its high Delta expression will suppress neighboring cells via their normal Notch, driving them to Type B.
