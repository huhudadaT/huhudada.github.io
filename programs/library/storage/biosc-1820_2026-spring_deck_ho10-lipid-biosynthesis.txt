Name: BIOSC 1820 HO10 Lipid Biosynthesis
Description: Fatty acid, phospholipid, sphingolipid, and cholesterol synthesis and their regulation.
Class: BIOSC 1820
Semester: Spring 2026

Q: What are the four main classes of lipids covered in HO10?
A: Fatty acids, phospholipids, sphingolipids, and cholesterol.

Q: What building block tag is attached before fatty acid synthesis?
A: ACP (acyl carrier protein).

Q: What enzyme converts acetyl CoA + HCO3- + ATP into malonyl CoA?
A: Acetyl CoA carboxylase (ACC).

Q: What are the substrates for acetyl CoA carboxylase?
A: Acetyl CoA, HCO3-, and ATP.

Q: What is the product of acetyl CoA carboxylase?
A: Malonyl CoA.

Q: What enzyme converts malonyl CoA to malonyl ACP?
A: Malonyl CoA-ACP transacylase.

Q: What cofactor/prosthetic group does acetyl CoA carboxylase carry?
A: Biotin (via biotin carrier protein, BCP).

Q: What are the two catalytic arms of acetyl CoA carboxylase?
A: Biotin carboxylase and transcarboxylase.

Q: What does biotin carboxylase do?
A: Attaches HCO3- as CO2 onto biotin, forming carboxybiotin.

Q: What does transcarboxylase do?
A: Transfers CO2 from carboxybiotin onto acetyl CoA.

Q: What does carboxybiotin refer to?
A: Biotin with CO2 attached.

Q: What does malonyl CoA inhibit, and why?
A: Carnitine acyltransferase, to prevent simultaneous fatty acid synthesis and breakdown.

Q: How does ACP structurally differ from CoA?
A: ACP is CoA without the AMP portion.

Q: Which phosphorylation state of ACC is active?
A: Dephosphorylated (active without P).

Q: Which phosphorylation state of ACC is inactive?
A: Phosphorylated (inactive with P).

Q: Where is fatty acid synthase located in animals?
A: In the cytosol.

Q: Where is fatty acid synthase located in plants?
A: In the chloroplast.

Q: How many active site thiols (SH) does fatty acid synthase have?
A: Two.

Q: How many carbons are added per cycle in fatty acid synthesis?
A: 2 carbons.

Q: How many cycles does it take to make palmitate (16C)?
A: 8 cycles.

Q: How many steps are in one fatty acid elongation cycle?
A: 4 steps.

Q: What is the final 16-carbon product of fatty acid synthase?
A: Palmitoyl CoA (then palmitic acid).

Q: Why does adding CO2 (malonyl group) make fatty acid synthesis favorable?
A: Decarboxylation provides the thermodynamic driving force.

Q: Where do the first 2 carbons of a fatty acid chain come from?
A: Acetyl CoA.

Q: In palmitate, which carbon positions are the first 2 carbons (from acetyl CoA)?
A: C15 and C16.

Q: Where do the other 14 carbons of palmitate come from?
A: 7 malonyl ACP units.

Q: What is the role of ACP in fatty acid synthesis?
A: It holds substrates firmly attached to the enzyme.

Q: What does malonyl ACP produce to prepare it for fatty acid chain extension?
A: An activated acyl group ready to be added to the chain.

Q: How many ATP are used to make 7 malonyl ACP?
A: 7 ATP.

Q: How many NADPH are used to make palmitate from 7 malonyl ACP?
A: 14 NADPH.

Q: Why must acetyl CoA be exported from the mitochondria for fatty acid synthesis?
A: Fatty acid synthesis occurs in the cytosol.

Q: What enzyme combines oxaloacetate + acetyl CoA into citrate?
A: Citrate synthase.

Q: How is citrate exported from the mitochondria?
A: Via the citrate transporter.

Q: What enzyme cleaves citrate into oxaloacetate + acetyl CoA in the cytosol?
A: Citrate lyase.

Q: What is oxaloacetate converted to for return to the mitochondria?
A: Malate.

Q: What transporter returns malate to the mitochondria?
A: The malate transporter.

Q: In the alternate shuttle pathway, what is malate converted to?
A: Pyruvate.

Q: What does the malate-to-pyruvate conversion provide for fatty acid synthesis?
A: NADPH.

Q: How does pyruvate return to the mitochondria?
A: Via the pyruvate transporter, where it forms oxaloacetate.

Q: What activates acetyl CoA carboxylase allosterically?
A: Citrate.

Q: What inhibits acetyl CoA carboxylase allosterically?
A: Palmitoyl CoA.

Q: Which hormone activates fatty acid synthesis?
A: Insulin.

Q: Which hormones inhibit fatty acid synthesis?
A: Glucagon and epinephrine.

Q: What combines with glycerol-3-phosphate and another fatty acid to form phosphatidic acid?
A: Palmitoyl CoA.

Q: What is the precursor to most phospholipids?
A: Phosphatidic acid.

Q: What activates phosphatidic acid for phospholipid synthesis?
A: CTP (forming CDP-diacylglycerol).

Q: What head groups can be added to CDP-diacylglycerol to form phospholipids?
A: G3P, serine, or inositol.

Q: What are the three precursors of sphingolipids?
A: Palmitoyl CoA, serine, and acetyl CoA.

Q: What molecule is formed from 3 acetyl CoA in cholesterol synthesis?
A: Mevalonate.

Q: What enzyme regulates mevalonate synthesis?
A: HMG-CoA reductase.

Q: What does mevalonate get converted to?
A: Isopentenyl pyrophosphate (IPP).

Q: Where does IPP formation occur?
A: In the cytosol.

Q: What does IPP form on the way to cholesterol?
A: Squalene.

Q: What two enzyme types convert squalene to cholesterol?
A: Oxygenase and cyclase.

Q: How many rings do sterols have?
A: Four.

Q: In what form is cholesterol stored?
A: As a cholesterol ester.

Q: How is cholesterol transported in the body?
A: Through the blood via lipoproteins.

Q: Which lipoprotein transports lipids from the intestine?
A: Chylomicrons.

Q: Where are LDL and HDL produced?
A: The liver.

Q: Why is LDL considered "bad"?
A: It causes artery damage.

Q: Why is HDL considered "good"?
A: It repairs artery damage.

Q: Name the key molecules to memorize for HO10.
A: Glycerol, acetate, acetyl group, acetyl CoA, malonyl CoA, malonyl ACP, HCO3-, palmitic acid, oxaloacetate, citrate, malate, pyruvate, glycerol-3-phosphate, CDP-diacylglycerol, cholesterol.

Q: What is the committed step of fatty acid synthesis?
A: Acetyl CoA → malonyl CoA (by ACC).

Q: What is the end product of one full round of fatty acid synthase?
A: Palmitate (16C).

Q: Draw: glycerol
A: 3-carbon chain with an -OH on each carbon (HOCH2-CHOH-CH2OH).

Q: Draw: acetate
A: CH3-COO- (2-carbon carboxylate: methyl group + deprotonated carboxyl).

Q: Draw: acetyl group
A: CH3-C(=O)- (2-carbon: methyl bonded to a carbonyl carbon).

Q: Draw: acetyl CoA
A: CH3-C(=O)-S-CoA (acetyl group joined to CoA through a thioester bond).

Q: Draw: malonyl CoA
A: HOOC-CH2-C(=O)-S-CoA (3C: carboxyl + CH2 + thioester to CoA).

Q: Draw: malonyl ACP
A: HOOC-CH2-C(=O)-S-ACP (malonyl group thioester-linked to ACP instead of CoA).

Q: Draw: bicarbonate (HCO3-)
A: Central C double-bonded to one O, single-bonded to two O's (one with H, one negative). Trigonal planar.

Q: Draw: palmitic acid
A: CH3-(CH2)14-COOH (straight 16-carbon saturated fatty acid with a terminal carboxyl).

Q: Draw: oxaloacetate
A: HOOC-C(=O)-CH2-COOH (4C: carboxyl – ketone – CH2 – carboxyl).

Q: Draw: citrate
A: 6C tricarboxylate: central C with -OH and -COO-, flanked by two CH2-COO- arms (HOOC-CH2-C(OH)(COOH)-CH2-COOH).

Q: Draw: malate
A: HOOC-CH(OH)-CH2-COOH (4C: carboxyl – CHOH – CH2 – carboxyl).

Q: Draw: pyruvate
A: CH3-C(=O)-COO- (3C: methyl – ketone – carboxylate).

Q: Draw: glycerol-3-phosphate
A: Glycerol backbone (HOCH2-CHOH-CH2-) with a phosphate group (-O-PO3^2-) on C3.

Q: Draw: CDP-diacylglycerol
A: Glycerol with two fatty acyl chains (ester-linked at C1 and C2) and CDP (cytidine diphosphate) linked through a phosphate at C3.

Q: Draw: cholesterol
A: Four fused rings (3 six-membered + 1 five-membered = steroid nucleus), -OH on ring A (C3), methyl groups at C10 and C13, and an 8-carbon isooctyl side chain off C17.
