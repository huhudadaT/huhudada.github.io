Name: Kidney Pathway and Function
Description: Renal blood and filtrate pathways, nephron segments, and the kidney's regulatory functions.
Class: BIOENG 1052
Semester: Fall 2026

Q: What vessel delivers blood to the kidney?
A: The renal artery.

Q: What is the functional unit of the kidney?
A: The nephron.

Q: Which arteriole carries blood into the glomerulus?
A: The afferent arteriole.

Q: Which arteriole carries blood out of the glomerulus?
A: The efferent arteriole.

Q: What capillary bed surrounds the renal tubules?
A: The peritubular capillaries.

Q: Where in the nephron does filtration occur?
A: The glomerulus.

Q: What structure collects filtrate leaving the glomerulus?
A: Bowman's capsule.

Q: Which tubule segment receives filtrate from Bowman's capsule?
A: The proximal convoluted tubule.

Q: Which nephron segment reabsorbs the most filtered water and solute?
A: The proximal convoluted tubule.

Q: Which limb of the loop of Henle is permeable to water?
A: The descending limb.

Q: Which limb of the loop of Henle actively transports NaCl out?
A: The thick ascending limb.

Q: What does the distal convoluted tubule drain into?
A: The collecting duct.

Q: What chamber do the major calyces drain into?
A: The renal pelvis.

Q: What tube carries urine from the kidney to the bladder?
A: The ureter.

Q: What are the three basic renal processes?
A: Filtration, reabsorption, and secretion.

Q: Which hormone increases water reabsorption in the collecting duct?
A: ADH (vasopressin).

Q: Which hormone drives sodium reabsorption in the distal nephron?
A: Aldosterone.

Q: What enzyme do the kidneys release when blood pressure falls?
A: Renin.

Q: Which kidney hormone stimulates red blood cell production?
A: Erythropoietin.

Q: Which vitamin do the kidneys activate?
A: Vitamin D.
