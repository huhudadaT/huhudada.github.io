Q: Air enters the nasal cavity and mouth, both of which lead to the ____, a muscular tube that is a common passageway for food and air.
*A. pharynx
B. trachea
C. larynx
D. alveoli
E. bronchi
Explain: The pharynx is the shared passage for the respiratory and digestive tracts; the larynx and everything below it carry air only.

Q: Which of the following structures is found in the respiratory zone of the airways?
A. pharynx
B. larynx
C. trachea
D. bronchioles
*E. alveolar sacs
Explain: The respiratory zone (where gas exchange occurs) starts at the respiratory bronchioles and includes alveolar ducts and sacs; the pharynx through terminal bronchioles are conducting zone.

Q: Which of the following pressures associated with ventilation is always negative?
A. atmospheric pressure
B. alveolar pressure
*C. intrapleural pressure
D. both atmospheric and alveolar pressures
E. atmospheric, alveolar, and intrapleural pressures
Explain: Intrapleural pressure stays below atmospheric throughout the breathing cycle; alveolar pressure swings both above and below atmospheric.

Q: Which respiratory muscles do you contract when forcefully blowing out the candles on a birthday cake?
A. the diaphragm and external intercostal muscles
B. the sternocleidomastoid and the scalene muscles
*C. the abdominal and internal intercostal muscles
D. the diaphragm and internal intercostal muscles
Explain: Forced expiration is active — abdominal muscles and internal intercostals push air out; the diaphragm and external intercostals drive inspiration.

Q: A patient with pulmonary edema must generate a bigger pressure gradient to fill the same lung volume. Pulmonary edema
A. increases lung compliance
B. does not affect lung compliance
*C. decreases lung compliance
D. None of the choices is correct.
Explain: Needing a larger pressure change for the same volume means the lung is stiffer — that is lower compliance.

Q: Secretion of surfactant by type II alveolar cells causes a marked ____ in lung surface tension to ____ the work required to breathe.
*A. decrease; decrease
B. increase; increase
C. decrease; increase
D. increase; increase
Explain: Surfactant lowers alveolar surface tension, which reduces the effort needed to inflate the lungs and prevents alveolar collapse.

Q: Which volume is the air inspired or expired during a single breathing cycle under resting conditions?
A. residual volume (RV)
B. expiratory reserve volume (ERV)
C. inspiratory reserve volume (IRV)
*D. tidal volume (VT)
Explain: Tidal volume is the air moved in one normal resting breath (about 500 mL).

Q: Which is the maximum volume of air that can be inspired after a normal expiration?
A. functional residual capacity (FRC)
*B. inspiratory capacity (IC)
C. vital capacity (VC)
D. total lung capacity (TLC)
Explain: Inspiratory capacity (IC = TV + IRV) is the most air you can inhale starting from the end of a normal resting expiration.

Q: Which of the following would promote the exchange of gases between two compartments?
*A. increasing the partial pressure difference of the gases between the two compartments
B. decreasing the surface area available for gas exchange
C. increasing the diffusion distance between the two compartments
D. None of the choices would promote gas exchange.
Explain: By Fick's law, diffusion rises with a larger partial-pressure gradient and larger surface area, and falls with greater distance.

Q: Approximately what percentage of inhaled oxygen is dissolved in blood plasma, with the remainder bound to hemoglobin?
*A. 1.5%
B. 5%
C. 10%
D. 30%
E. 45%
Explain: Only about 1.5% of blood O2 is dissolved in plasma; roughly 98.5% is carried bound to hemoglobin.

Q: The greatest percentage of CO2 is transported in the blood
A. bound to the surface of erythrocytes
B. as dissolved CO2 in the plasma
C. as carbaminohemoglobin
*D. as bicarbonate ions
E. bound to carbonic anhydrase
Explain: About 70% of CO2 travels as bicarbonate (HCO3-); roughly 23% as carbaminohemoglobin and 7% dissolved.

Q: Which of the following would make the oxygen-hemoglobin dissociation curve shift right?
*A. increased H+ concentration
B. increased pH
C. decreased temperature
D. decreased CO2 concentration
E. None of the choices is correct.
Explain: A right shift (lower O2 affinity, easier unloading) is caused by increased H+, CO2, temperature, and BPG — the Bohr effect.

Q: Which of the following statements about hemoglobin is TRUE?
*A. Oxyhemoglobin and deoxyhemoglobin differ markedly in their affinity for oxygen.
B. BPG (2,3-bisphosphoglycerate) raises the oxygen affinity of hemoglobin.
C. Both H+ and CO2 inhibit the release of bound oxygen.
D. The binding of oxygen to a hemoglobin molecule diminishes the binding of additional oxygen.
Explain: The oxygenated (R) and deoxygenated (T) states have very different O2 affinities; BPG lowers affinity, H+/CO2 promote O2 release, and O2 binding increases affinity for more O2.

Q: Which of the following structures generates the basic respiratory rhythm?
A. the cerebral cortex
B. the pneumotaxic area in the upper pons
C. the apneustic area in the lower pons
*D. the pre-Botzinger complex in the ventral respiratory group (VRG)
E. the diaphragm and the external intercostal muscles
Explain: The pre-Botzinger complex in the medulla's ventral respiratory group is the pacemaker that sets the basic breathing rhythm.

Q: Which of the following statements about the control of respiration is FALSE?
*A. The only output from the medullary respiratory centers is via the phrenic nerve.
B. Information from the cerebral cortex can override the medullary respiratory center.
C. The normal rhythmic pattern of breathing originates from neurons in the pre-Botzinger complex.
D. The respiratory muscles have no intrinsic rhythmical activity.
Explain: False — the medullary centers also drive the intercostal muscles via the intercostal nerves, not only the diaphragm via the phrenic nerve.

Q: Dalton's law states that
A. the volume of gas that dissolves in a solvent is proportional to its solubility and gas pressure.
B. gas volume and temperature are directly proportional.
C. gas volume and pressure are inversely proportional.
*D. in a mixture of gases, the total pressure is the sum of the individual partial pressures.
E. None of the answers are correct.
Explain: Dalton's law: in a gas mixture, total pressure equals the sum of each gas's partial pressure. Option A describes Henry's law.

Q: Boyle's law states that gas volume is
A. inversely proportional to temperature.
B. directly proportional to pressure.
*C. inversely proportional to pressure.
D. directly proportional to temperature.
E. None of the answers are correct.
Explain: Boyle's law: at constant temperature, gas volume and pressure are inversely proportional — the basis of airflow during breathing.

Q: A typical value for intrapleural pressure is ____ mm Hg relative to atmospheric pressure.
A. +4
B. -760
C. 0
*D. -4
E. +760
Explain: Intrapleural pressure is about -4 mm Hg relative to atmospheric at rest, keeping the lungs inflated against the chest wall.

Q: When the diaphragm, external intercostal, and scalene muscles contract,
A. intrapulmonary pressure increases.
*B. intrapleural pressure decreases.
C. the volume of the lungs decreases.
D. expiration occurs.
E. All of the answers are correct.
Explain: These are inspiratory muscles; their contraction expands the thorax, making intrapleural pressure more negative and drawing air in.

Q: An increase in PCO2 would cause the bronchioles to
*A. dilate and the systemic arterioles to dilate.
B. constrict and the systemic arterioles to dilate.
C. constrict and the systemic arterioles to constrict.
D. dilate and the systemic arterioles to constrict.
E. None of the answers are correct.
Explain: High CO2 dilates airway bronchioles to boost ventilation to that region, and dilates systemic arterioles to increase tissue perfusion.

Q: Under what condition is lung compliance increased?
*A. Emphysema
B. Asthma
C. Prolonged shallow breathing
D. Respiratory distress syndrome of the newborn
E. Cystic fibrosis
Explain: Emphysema destroys elastic lung tissue, so the lungs become floppy and easier to inflate — increased compliance. The others reduce it.

Q: The local matching of blood flow with ventilation is called
*A. ventilation-perfusion coupling
B. the Haldane effect
C. the Bohr effect
D. chloride shifting
Explain: Ventilation-perfusion coupling adjusts airway and vessel diameter so blood flow matches airflow in each lung region.

Q: What is the tidal volume of the lungs?
A. the maximum volume that can be inspired after a normal inspiration
B. the maximum volume that can be expired after a maximum inspiration
C. the volume that remains in the lungs after a maximum expiration
*D. the volume of air inspired or expired during a single breathing cycle under resting conditions
E. the maximum volume that can be expired after a normal expiration
Explain: Tidal volume is the air moved in and out during one normal resting breath. A is IRV, B is vital capacity, C is residual volume, E is ERV.

Q: Which of the following correctly represents the formula for the maximum volume of air the lungs can hold?
A. TLC = VC + ERV
B. VC = VT + RV
C. FEV1 = VC + 1.0
*D. TLC = VC + RV
Explain: Total lung capacity = vital capacity + residual volume; RV cannot be exhaled, so it must be added to VC to get the true maximum.

Q: Which of the following is NOT an obstructive pulmonary disorder?
A. Asthma
*B. Tuberculosis
C. COPD
D. Emphysema
E. Chronic bronchitis
Explain: Tuberculosis is an infectious/restrictive process, not an obstructive one; asthma, COPD, emphysema, and chronic bronchitis all obstruct airflow.

Q: Which of the following is NOT a function of the kidneys?
A. excrete wastes from the body
B. regulate blood ionic composition
C. regulate blood pH
*D. regulate body temperature
E. produce hormones including calcitriol and erythropoietin
Explain: Body-temperature regulation is not a kidney function; the kidneys excrete wastes, balance ions and pH, and make calcitriol and erythropoietin.

Q: The renal corpuscle, the initial blood-filtering component of a nephron, is composed of
A. Bowman's capsule and a proximal tubule
B. a glomerulus and a proximal tubule
*C. a glomerulus and Bowman's capsule
D. the descending and ascending limbs of the loop of Henle
E. Bowman's capsule and a distal tubule
Explain: The renal corpuscle is the glomerulus (capillary tuft) plus the surrounding Bowman's capsule, where filtration occurs.

Q: Each nephron has one ____ that carries blood to the glomerulus and one ____ that carries blood away from the glomerulus.
A. renal arteriole; renal venule
B. efferent arteriole; afferent arteriole
C. afferent capillary; efferent capillary
*D. afferent arteriole; efferent arteriole
E. afferent venule; efferent venule
Explain: Afferent Arrives (blood in), Efferent Exits (blood out); both are arterioles, and the glomerulus sits between them. Note option B has the pair reversed.

Q: Which is the correct pathway for filtrate to flow through the nephron? (1 distal tubule, 2 Bowman's capsule, 3 collecting duct, 4 proximal tubule, 5 descending limb, 6 ascending limb)
*A. 2 to 4 to 5 to 6 to 1 to 3
B. 2 to 1 to 6 to 5 to 4 to 3
C. 5 to 4 to 6 to 1 to 3 to 2
D. 2 to 4 to 1 to 6 to 3 to 5
E. 3 to 1 to 6 to 5 to 4 to 2
Explain: Filtrate flows Bowman's capsule to proximal tubule to descending limb to ascending limb to distal tubule to collecting duct.

Q: Which of the following is NOT a basic function performed by nephrons?
A. filtration at the glomerulus
*B. filtration at the renal tubules
C. secretion into the renal tubules
D. reabsorption from the renal tubules
Explain: Filtration happens only at the glomerulus, not at the tubules; the tubules perform reabsorption and secretion.

Q: In what order do filtered substances cross the three barriers of the filtration membrane?
*A. through fenestrations in glomerular endothelial cells, then the basement membrane, then filtration slits formed by pedicels
B. through the basement membrane, then endothelial fenestrations, then filtration slits
C. through filtration slits, then the basement membrane, then endothelial fenestrations
D. None of the choices is correct.
Explain: Order: fenestrated glomerular endothelium, then basement membrane, then the filtration slits between podocyte pedicels.

Q: Which pressure that determines glomerular filtration has a value of 0 mmHg in a healthy kidney under normal conditions?
A. glomerular capillary hydrostatic pressure (PGC)
B. Bowman's space hydrostatic pressure (PBS)
C. plasma colloid osmotic pressure (piGC)
*D. Bowman's space colloid osmotic pressure (piBS)
Explain: Normally almost no protein is filtered, so fluid in Bowman's space exerts essentially no colloid osmotic pressure (piBS is about 0).

Q: Which pressure is due to proteins such as albumin, globulins, and fibrinogen in glomerular plasma and opposes filtration?
A. glomerular capillary hydrostatic pressure (PGC)
B. Bowman's space hydrostatic pressure (PBS)
*C. plasma colloid osmotic pressure (piGC)
D. Bowman's space colloid osmotic pressure (piBS)
Explain: Plasma colloid osmotic pressure, created by blood proteins, pulls water back into the capillary and opposes filtration.

Q: Which of the following statements about glomerular filtration rate (GFR) is FALSE?
A. GFR is the amount of filtrate formed in all renal corpuscles of both kidneys each minute.
B. GFR increases very little when systemic blood pressure rises above normal.
C. GFR is regulated by adjusting blood flow into and out of the glomerulus.
*D. GFR is increased by constriction of the afferent arteriole.
E. GFR is regulated by angiotensin II and atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP).
Explain: False — constricting the afferent arteriole reduces blood flow into the glomerulus and lowers GFR, not raises it.

Q: Which statement about a substance X present in the urine is correct?
A. It must be filterable in the glomerulus.
B. It must not have been filtered in the glomerulus.
C. It must have been secreted into the renal tubules.
*D. It may either have been filtered in the glomerulus or secreted into the renal tubules.
Explain: Anything appearing in urine got there by filtration and/or tubular secretion, so either route or both is possible.

Q: Which of the following statements regarding antidiuretic hormone (ADH) is TRUE?
A. ADH is released by the adrenal cortex.
B. ADH inhibits reabsorption of water into the blood.
C. ADH release is stimulated when the body is overhydrated.
*D. ADH increases water permeability of principal cells in the late distal tubule and throughout the collecting duct.
Explain: ADH from the posterior pituitary inserts aquaporins in principal cells to increase water reabsorption; it is released when the body is dehydrated.

Q: Which of the following is NOT a hormone that acts on the renal tubules to regulate water and ion homeostasis?
A. antidiuretic hormone (ADH)
B. aldosterone
*C. insulin
D. atrial natriuretic peptide (ANP)
E. parathyroid hormone
Explain: Insulin regulates blood glucose, not renal water/ion handling; ADH, aldosterone, ANP, and PTH all act on the tubules.

Q: A highly concentrated urine can form because the interstitial fluid surrounding the collecting ducts is ____ the filtrate within the collecting ducts.
A. hypoosmotic relative to
B. isosmotic with
*C. hyperosmotic relative to
D. in direct contact with
Explain: The medullary interstitium is hyperosmotic to the collecting-duct filtrate, so with ADH water is drawn out and urine becomes concentrated.

Q: Which of the following statements regarding the loop of Henle is TRUE?
A. The ascending limb is highly permeable to water.
B. The descending limb is impermeable to water.
C. The ascending limb is impermeable to solutes.
*D. The descending limb is impermeable to solutes.
Explain: The descending limb is permeable to water but not solutes; the ascending limb is the opposite — impermeable to water while it moves solutes out.

Q: Which of the following statements concerning the micturition reflex is FALSE? A full bladder
A. stimulates parasympathetic activity to contract the detrusor muscle.
B. inhibits sympathetic activity to the internal urethral sphincter, causing it to relax.
C. inhibits somatic motor neurons to the external urethral sphincter, causing it to relax.
D. causes a sensation of fullness that triggers a conscious desire to urinate before the reflex occurs.
*E. All of the statements are true.
Explain: All four statements are correct, so none is false — the reflex combines parasympathetic detrusor contraction with relaxation of both sphincters.

Q: The long loop of Henle functions as a countercurrent multiplier.
*A. True
B. False
Explain: True — the long loops of juxtamedullary nephrons actively establish the medullary osmotic gradient, the countercurrent multiplier.

Q: Which of the following plays a role in promoting reabsorption of water from the tubular fluid of the collecting duct?
A. renal autoregulation
B. functional incontinence
C. micturition reflex
D. renal plasma clearance
*E. urea recycling
Explain: Urea recycling adds urea to the medullary interstitium, raising its osmolarity and pulling water out of the collecting duct.

Q: If the renal plasma clearance of a substance equals the glomerular filtration rate, the substance is
A. filtered and undergoes net secretion
B. filtered and undergoes net reabsorption
*C. filtered but neither secreted nor reabsorbed
D. filtered, secreted, and reabsorbed
E. not filtered
Explain: Clearance equal to GFR (like inulin) means all filtered substance is excreted unchanged, with no net reabsorption or secretion.

Q: An alternative to administering inulin to determine the glomerular filtration rate is measuring the
*A. creatinine clearance
B. level of serum creatinine
C. level of blood urea nitrogen
D. renal plasma flow
E. level of insulin
Explain: Creatinine is produced at a steady rate and largely filtered, so creatinine clearance approximates GFR without infused inulin.

Q: During renal autoregulation, the glomerulus receives feedback from
A. juxtaglomerular cells
B. podocytes
C. smooth muscle cells
*D. macula densa
E. mesangial cells
Explain: The macula densa senses tubular NaCl and signals the afferent arteriole (tubuloglomerular feedback) to adjust GFR.

Q: Angiotensin II increases the glomerular filtration rate by causing vasoconstriction of both the afferent and efferent arterioles.
A. True
*B. False
Explain: False — angiotensin II preferentially constricts the efferent arteriole; strong constriction of the afferent would instead lower GFR.

Q: In the late distal tubule, reabsorption of Na+ and secretion of K+ is done by these cells:
A. intercalated cells
*B. principal cells
C. mesangial cells
D. smooth muscle cells
E. endothelial cells
Explain: Principal cells reabsorb Na+ (and water via ADH) and secrete K+ under aldosterone control; intercalated cells handle acid-base balance.

Q: Aquaporin-1 is the water channel protein present in the apical membranes of principal cells.
A. True
*B. False
Explain: False — principal cells use aquaporin-2 (ADH-regulated) apically; aquaporin-1 is found in the proximal tubule and descending limb.

Q: Juxtamedullary nephrons lie deep in the cortex close to the medulla and have a short loop of Henle.
A. True
*B. False
Explain: False — juxtamedullary nephrons have long loops of Henle that dip deep into the medulla; cortical nephrons have short loops.

Q: In a healthy kidney, very little of which substance is filtered by the glomerulus and ends up in the filtrate?
A. glucose
B. Na+, K+, Cl-
C. vitamins
D. amino acids
*E. proteins
Explain: The filtration membrane holds back plasma proteins, so almost none appear in the filtrate; small solutes filter freely.

Q: What is the osmolarity of the filtrate in the cortex?
*A. 300 mOsmol/L
B. 600 mOsmol/L
C. 900 mOsmol/L
D. 1200 mOsmol/L
E. 3000 mOsmol/L
Explain: In the cortex the interstitium and filtrate are about 300 mOsmol/L, near plasma; osmolarity rises deeper in the medulla, up to about 1200.

Q: Which of the following is NOT a feature of a countercurrent mechanism?
A. helps maintain the concentration gradient
B. helps make the passage of water easier
C. helps concentrate the urine
*D. helps dilute the urine
Explain: The countercurrent mechanism builds a medullary gradient to concentrate the urine, not dilute it.

Q: Countercurrent exchange is the passive exchange of solutes and water between the blood of the vasa recta and the medullary interstitial fluid as a result of countercurrent flow.
*A. True
B. False
Explain: True — the vasa recta act as countercurrent exchangers, preserving the medullary gradient while carrying off reabsorbed water.

Q: Countercurrent multiplication is the formation of a progressively increasing osmotic gradient in the medullary interstitial fluid as a result of countercurrent flow.
*A. True
B. False
Explain: True — the loop of Henle actively multiplies a small transverse gradient into a large corticomedullary osmotic gradient.

Q: At the end of meiosis I, ____ daughter cells are produced that are genetically ____ each other and the parent cell.
*A. two; distinct from
B. two; identical to
C. four; distinct from
D. four; identical to
Explain: Meiosis I yields two haploid daughter cells that differ genetically due to crossing over and independent assortment.

Q: Genetic variation results from crossing-over between ____ chromatids of a ____ pair of chromosomes.
*A. nonsister; homologous
B. sister; homologous
C. nonsister; nonhomologous
D. sister; nonhomologous
Explain: Crossing over swaps segments between nonsister chromatids of homologous chromosomes, creating new allele combinations.

Q: Normal sperm production requires a temperature about ____ core body temperature.
A. 10 degrees below
*B. 3 degrees C below
C. equal to
D. 3 degrees C above
E. 10 degrees C above
Explain: The scrotum keeps the testes about 2 to 3 degrees C below core temperature, which is required for normal spermatogenesis.

Q: Within the seminiferous tubule, ____ are joined by tight junctions to form the blood-testis barrier.
A. primary spermatocytes
B. secondary spermatocytes
*C. Sertoli cells
D. spermatids
E. sperm cells
Explain: Sertoli cells are joined by tight junctions to form the blood-testis barrier, protecting developing sperm from the immune system.

Q: The hormone inhibin is released from ____ to inhibit ____ secretion by the anterior pituitary, suppressing spermatogenesis.
A. Sertoli cells; luteinizing hormone (LH)
*B. Sertoli cells; follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH)
C. Leydig cells; luteinizing hormone (LH)

Q: Primary oocytes enter ____ of meiosis I during ____ but do not complete that phase until after puberty.
*A. prophase; fetal development
B. prophase; early childhood
C. metaphase; fetal development
D. anaphase; early childhood
E. anaphase; early puberty
Explain: Oocytes arrest in prophase I during fetal development and stay arrested until stimulated, cycle by cycle, after puberty.

Q: During oogenesis, meiosis II is completed upon ____, generating an ovum in the ____.
A. puberty; ovary
B. ovulation; ovary
C. ovulation; fallopian tube
*D. fertilization; fallopian tube
E. fertilization; uterus
Explain: The secondary oocyte only finishes meiosis II if fertilized, and this happens in the uterine (fallopian) tube.

Q: Which hormone, secreted by which gland, triggers ovulation and promotes corpus luteum formation?
A. gonadotropin-releasing hormone; anterior pituitary
*B. luteinizing hormone; anterior pituitary
C. follicle-stimulating hormone; hypothalamus
D. luteinizing hormone; hypothalamus
E. follicle-stimulating hormone; anterior pituitary
Explain: A surge of luteinizing hormone from the anterior pituitary triggers ovulation and converts the follicle into the corpus luteum.

Q: Which hormone stimulates endometrial glands to secrete glycogen and lipids for a fertilized egg and, at high levels, inhibits release of GnRH, FSH, and LH?
*A. progesterone
B. relaxin
C. inhibin
D. beta-estradiol
Explain: Progesterone from the corpus luteum prepares the endometrium for implantation and gives negative feedback to the hypothalamic-pituitary axis.

Q: Which phase of the human sexual response described by Masters and Johnson is unique to males?
A. the excitement phase
B. the plateau phase
C. the orgasm phase
D. the resolution phase
*E. None of the choices is correct.
Explain: All four phases occur in both sexes; the refractory period is typical of males but is not one of the listed phases, so none is unique.

Q: A genetically male embryo develops testes because

Q: Which of the following statements regarding puberty is FALSE?

Q: Sperm can survive up to ____ in the female reproductive tract, while a secondary oocyte is viable for about ____ after ovulation.

*D. 5 days; 24 hours

Q: Which mechanisms act to prevent polyspermy? Fusion of a sperm cell with a secondary oocyte triggers

Q: Which of the following statements regarding embryonic development is correct?

Q: Which of the following statements regarding the placenta is correct?

B. It develops entirely from maternal tissue.
*C. It is the site of nutrient and waste exchange between the mother and the fetus.

Explain: The placenta exchanges nutrients, gases, and wastes; maternal and fetal blood do not mix, and O2/nutrients move from mother to fetus.

Q: Which of the following statements regarding the hormonal control of lactation is FALSE?

D. Oxytocin secretion from the posterior pituitary increases in response to suckling.
Explain: False — during pregnancy high estrogen and progesterone block prolactin's milk-making effect; production begins only after they fall postpartum.

Q: Which of the following results from a vasectomy, in which a portion of each vas deferens is removed?

*B. Sperm can no longer reach the urethra.
C. Blood testosterone levels are diminished.
D. Sexual desire and performance are decreased.
E. Ejaculation is prevented.
Explain: Cutting the vas deferens blocks sperm from reaching the urethra; sperm production, testosterone, libido, and ejaculation are unaffected.

Q: Which of the following statements regarding hormonal contraceptive methods is correct?

*B. Progestin thickens cervical mucus, making it difficult for sperm to reach the egg.

Explain: Progestin thickens cervical mucus and thins the endometrium. Hormonal contraceptives suppress (not promote) GnRH/FSH/LH, and are generally more effective than barrier methods.

Q: Which fertility-expanding technique fertilizes an egg in vitro by suctioning a sperm or spermatid from the testis and injecting it into the egg's cytoplasm?

*B. intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI)
C. embryo transfer
D. gamete intrafallopian transfer (GIFT)
Explain: ICSI injects a single sperm directly into the oocyte cytoplasm, used especially for male-factor infertility.

Q: Which term describes the collapse of a lung or part of a lung due to loss of air in the alveoli?
A. Pneumothorax
B. Emphysema
*C. Atelectasis
D. Bronchitis
Explain: Atelectasis is alveolar collapse; a pneumothorax (air in the pleural space) is one cause of it.

Q: A pneumothorax is best defined as
*A. air in the pleural cavity
B. fluid in the alveoli
C. destruction of alveolar walls
D. narrowing of the bronchioles
Explain: A pneumothorax is air in the pleural space, which breaks the seal that keeps the lung inflated and can collapse it.

Q: Which condition is a chronic obstructive disease marked by destruction of alveolar walls and loss of elastic recoil?
A. Asthma
B. Tuberculosis
C. Atelectasis
*D. Emphysema
Explain: Emphysema destroys alveolar walls, reducing surface area and elastic recoil, which increases compliance but traps air.

Q: Asthma is characterized by
A. permanent alveolar destruction
*B. reversible bronchoconstriction and airway inflammation
C. air in the pleural space
D. thick mucus from a CFTR defect
Explain: Asthma involves reversible airway narrowing from bronchospasm and inflammation, unlike the permanent damage of emphysema.

Q: COPD most commonly refers to the combination of
A. asthma and pneumonia
B. tuberculosis and atelectasis
*C. emphysema and chronic bronchitis
D. cystic fibrosis and RDS
Explain: Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease is typically emphysema plus chronic bronchitis, both causing airflow obstruction.

Q: Chronic bronchitis is defined clinically by
*A. a productive cough with excess mucus over an extended period
B. alveolar wall destruction
C. collapse of a lobe of the lung
D. air in the pleural cavity
Explain: Chronic bronchitis is chronic airway inflammation with mucus hypersecretion and a persistent productive cough.

Q: Cystic fibrosis results from a defect that causes
A. autoimmune destruction of Sertoli cells
B. loss of alveolar elastin
C. inadequate surfactant in a newborn
*D. abnormally thick, sticky secretions that clog the airways
Explain: A CFTR chloride-channel mutation produces thick mucus that obstructs airways and pancreatic ducts, promoting infection.

Q: Respiratory distress syndrome of the newborn is caused primarily by
A. a CFTR mutation
*B. insufficient pulmonary surfactant
C. air in the pleural space
D. carbon monoxide exposure
Explain: Premature infants lack adequate surfactant, so alveolar surface tension is high and the alveoli tend to collapse.

Q: Carbon monoxide poisoning impairs oxygen delivery because CO acts by
A. blocking the trachea
B. destroying alveolar walls
*C. binding hemoglobin far more tightly than oxygen
D. dissolving surfactant
Explain: CO binds hemoglobin with roughly 200 times the affinity of O2, forming carboxyhemoglobin and blocking O2 transport.

Q: High-altitude sickness occurs mainly because of
*A. reduced partial pressure of oxygen in inspired air
B. increased barometric pressure
C. excess surfactant
D. carbon monoxide accumulation
Explain: At altitude the lower atmospheric pressure lowers the partial pressure of inspired O2, causing hypoxia even though the percentage of oxygen is unchanged.

Q: Which statement correctly contrasts obstructive and restrictive lung disease?
A. Obstructive limits expansion; restrictive limits airflow out.
B. Both limit only expiration.
C. Both limit only inspiration.
*D. Obstructive limits airflow out; restrictive limits lung expansion.
Explain: Obstructive diseases (asthma, COPD) impede airflow, especially expiration; restrictive diseases (fibrosis, RDS) limit lung expansion and volume.

Q: Tuberculosis is
A. an obstructive airway disease
*B. an infectious lung disease caused by a bacterium
C. air in the pleural cavity
D. a surfactant deficiency
Explain: TB is caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis and behaves as an infectious/restrictive process, not an obstructive one.

Q: Azotemia refers to
A. blood in the urine
B. protein in the urine
*C. elevated nitrogenous wastes such as urea in the blood
D. yellowing of the skin
Explain: Azotemia is a buildup of nitrogen-containing wastes (urea, creatinine) in the blood, usually from impaired kidney filtration.

Q: Uremia is best described as
*A. a toxic clinical syndrome from severe retention of nitrogenous wastes
B. a mild rise in blood urea with no symptoms
C. glucose in the urine
D. inflammation of the bladder
Explain: Uremia is the symptomatic, toxic state of advanced kidney failure with marked accumulation of wastes.

Q: Hematuria means
A. protein in the urine
B. wastes in the blood
C. pus in the urine
*D. blood in the urine
Explain: Hematuria is the presence of blood (red blood cells) in the urine; do not confuse it with proteinuria.

Q: Proteinuria indicates
A. blood in the urine
*B. protein in the urine, often signaling glomerular damage
C. nitrogen wastes in the blood
D. bacteria in the bladder
Explain: Proteinuria is abnormal protein in the urine, commonly reflecting a leaky glomerular filtration barrier.

Q: Dialysis is a procedure that involves
A. surgically removing a kidney
B. dissolving kidney stones
*C. artificially filtering wastes and excess fluid from the blood
D. measuring the glomerular filtration rate
Explain: Dialysis uses an artificial membrane to remove wastes and fluid when the kidneys can no longer do so adequately.

Q: Which group are all inflammatory conditions of the urinary tract?
*A. pyelonephritis, cystitis, nephritis
B. emphysema, bronchitis, asthma
C. menarche, thelarche, climacteric
D. azotemia, uremia, jaundice
Explain: Pyelonephritis (kidney and its pelvis), cystitis (bladder), and nephritis (kidney) are all urinary inflammations; pyelitis is inflammation of the renal pelvis.

Q: Cystitis specifically refers to inflammation of
A. the kidney
B. the renal pelvis
*C. the urinary bladder
D. the urethra
Explain: Cystitis is inflammation of the bladder, most often caused by a urinary tract infection.

Q: Urinary stress incontinence is
A. the inability to sense a full bladder
B. a complete inability to urinate
C. blood in the urine
*D. leakage of urine when abdominal pressure rises, as in coughing or laughing
Explain: Stress incontinence is involuntary urine loss when abdominal pressure increases (coughing, sneezing, lifting), often from weak pelvic-floor support.

Q: Cryptorchidism is
*A. failure of one or both testes to descend into the scrotum
B. inflammation of the prostate
C. endometrial tissue outside the uterus
D. absence of menstruation
Explain: Cryptorchidism is an undescended testis; because sperm need cooler scrotal temperatures, it can impair fertility.

Q: Which of the following describes benign prostatic hyperplasia?
A. a cancerous growth of prostate cells
B. inflammation of the prostate from infection
*C. a non-cancerous enlargement of the prostate that can obstruct urine flow
D. failure of the prostate to develop
Explain: BPH is a benign enlargement of the prostate common with aging; prostatitis is inflammation and prostate cancer is malignant.

Q: Endometriosis is
A. inflammation of the endometrium from infection
*B. the presence of endometrial tissue outside the uterus
C. absence of the endometrium
D. cancer of the cervix
Explain: Endometriosis is endometrial-like tissue growing outside the uterus, such as on the ovaries or peritoneum, causing pain and sometimes infertility.

Q: Match these female reproductive milestones in order: menarche, thelarche, climacteric.
A. onset of breast development; first menstruation; first ovulation
B. the transition to menopause; first menstruation; onset of breast development
C. first ovulation; the transition to menopause; first menstruation
*D. first menstruation; onset of breast development; the transition to menopause
Explain: Menarche is the first menstrual period, thelarche is the beginning of breast development, and climacteric is the years-long transition to menopause.

Q: Jaundice is a yellowing of the skin and eyes caused by
*A. accumulation of bilirubin
B. excess urea in the blood
C. blood in the urine
D. low oxygen saturation
Explain: Jaundice results from elevated bilirubin from red blood cell breakdown, often signaling a liver or bile-duct problem.

Q: Regarding whether "Pap smear" is an acronym, the Pap smear is
A. an acronym for "Papillary Analysis Procedure"
B. an acronym for a "Pelvic and Perineal" exam
*C. named after Dr. Papanicolaou; a test that screens for cervical cell changes
D. a test that measures prostate antigens
Explain: "Pap" is not an acronym; it is short for Papanicolaou, who developed the cervical cytology screening test.
