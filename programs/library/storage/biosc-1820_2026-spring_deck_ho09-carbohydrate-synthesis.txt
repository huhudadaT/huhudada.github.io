Name: BIOSC 1820 HO09 Carbohydrate Synthesis
Description: The Calvin cycle, photorespiration, C4 metabolism, and sucrose and starch synthesis.
Class: BIOSC 1820
Semester: Spring 2026

Q: What process is the ultimate source of all carbohydrates in human metabolism?
A: Plant and bacterial anabolic processes (largely reductive, NAD(P)H-requiring, ATP-burning).

Q: How does anabolism differ from catabolism in terms of redox and energy?
A: Anabolism is reductive and burns ATP; catabolism is oxidative and produces ATP.

Q: What are the three plastid types and their functions?
A: Chloroplasts (carbon assimilation), amyloplasts (starch storage), proplastids (loss of internal membranes/chlorophyll).

Q: Are plastids interconvertible?
A: Yes — depends on sunlight, location, and energy needs.

Q: Where in the chloroplast does carbon fixation occur?
A: The stroma.

Q: What are three other names for the Calvin Cycle?
A: Carbon assimilation, carbon fixation, or the "Dark Reactions."

Q: Why is "Dark Reactions" a misleading name for the Calvin Cycle?
A: The reactions can occur in sunlight.

Q: What are the three stages of the Calvin Cycle?
A: Stage 1: fixation. Stage 2: reduction. Stage 3: regeneration of acceptor.

Q: What enzyme catalyzes the first committed step of the Calvin Cycle?
A: RuBisCO (ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase).

Q: What percentage of soluble chloroplast protein is RuBisCO?
A: ~50%.

Q: What is the subunit composition of RuBisCO?
A: 8 × 53 kDa subunits and 8 × 14 kDa subunits.

Q: What does RuBisCO need to assemble?
A: A chaperone.

Q: What two conditions activate RuBisCO?
A: High pH and Mg2+.

Q: What does high pH and Mg2+ signal to RuBisCO?
A: That the light reactions are ongoing.

Q: What is the ΔG of the RuBisCO reaction?
A: -52 kJ/mol (favorable).

Q: What residues/cofactors are at the RuBisCO active site?
A: A carbamoylated lysine and a Mg2+ ion.

Q: What are the 5 steps of the RuBisCO carboxylation mechanism?
A: Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate → enediol → 2-carboxy-3-keto intermediate → hydrated intermediate → 2x 3-phosphoglycerate.

Q: What does 3 ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate yield in the carboxylation step?
A: 6 molecules of 3-phosphoglycerate.

Q: What enzyme converts 3-phosphoglycerate to 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate?
A: Phosphoglycerate kinase.

Q: What enzyme converts 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate to glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate?
A: Glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase.

Q: How is Calvin cycle Stage 2 different from gluconeogenesis?
A: It uses NADPH instead of NADH.

Q: How many G3P molecules are produced per turn of the Calvin cycle (from 3 RuBP)?
A: 6 G3P.

Q: How many G3P molecules are recycled to regenerate RuBP?
A: 5 of 6 (5/6 are recycled).

Q: How many G3P molecules are exported per turn for sugar synthesis?
A: 1 of 6.

Q: How many ATP and NADPH are needed to fix one carbon at the G3P stage?
A: 2 ATP and 2 NADPH.

Q: What enzyme converts G3P to dihydroxyacetone phosphate?
A: Triose phosphate isomerase.

Q: What does transaldolase do?
A: Transfers a 3-carbon unit (acts on aldoses).

Q: What does transketolase do?
A: Transfers a 2-carbon unit (acts on ketoses).

Q: What is the final ATP-requiring step that drives Stage 3?
A: Ribulose-5-phosphate kinase (Ru5P → RuBP).

Q: How many NADPH and ATP are needed per triose phosphate produced?
A: 6 NADPH and 9 ATP.

Q: How many NADPH and ATP are needed per hexose produced?
A: 12 NADPH and 18 ATP.

Q: What is the overall net equation for hexose synthesis in the Calvin cycle?
A: 6 CO2 + 18 ATP + 12 NADPH → C6H12O6 + 18 ADP + 18 Pi + 12 NADP+.

Q: Why must triose phosphates be exported from the stroma?
A: Sucrose synthesis occurs in the cytosol; the chloroplast membrane is impermeable to hexoses.

Q: What transporter exchanges triose phosphates and Pi across the chloroplast membrane?
A: The Pi-triose phosphate antiporter.

Q: What is thioredoxin?
A: A small disulfide-containing protein that regulates Calvin cycle enzymes.

Q: What reduces thioredoxin?
A: Reduced ferredoxin (which has escaped from the electron transport chain).

Q: Which protein actually contains iron — thioredoxin or ferredoxin?
A: Ferredoxin (thioredoxin contains a disulfide, S-S).

Q: What four Calvin cycle enzymes are regulated by thioredoxin?
A: Ribulose-5-phosphate kinase, fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase, sedoheptulose-1,7-bisphosphatase, and glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase.

Q: What does thioredoxin signal to the plant?
A: That light reactions are running — make starch/sucrose by day, eat sugars at night.

Q: What does the oxygenase activity of RuBisCO produce?
A: 3-phosphoglycerate + phosphoglycolate.

Q: How much higher is RuBisCO's Km for O2 than for CO2?
A: ~40-fold higher.

Q: Why does the oxygenase reaction occur despite the high Km for O2?
A: When O2 concentration is high enough, the reaction proceeds.

Q: What are the three downsides of photorespiration?
A: Wasted RuBisCO, loss of ATP, and loss of previously fixed CO2.

Q: How do cyanobacteria avoid photorespiration?
A: They concentrate CO2 in carboxysomes.

Q: What is the 8-step glycolate pathway from phosphoglycolate to 3-phosphoglycerate?
A: Phosphoglycolate → glycolate → glyoxylate → glycine → serine → hydroxypyruvate → glycerate → 3-phosphoglycerate.

Q: What three organelles are involved in the glycolate pathway?
A: Chloroplast, peroxisome, and mitochondrion.

Q: What two peroxisomal enzymes act in the glycolate pathway?
A: Glycolic acid oxidase and α-hydroxy acid reductase.

Q: What enzyme converts 2 glycine to serine + CO2 in the mitochondrion?
A: Glycine decarboxylase.

Q: What cofactors does glycine decarboxylase use?
A: THF and PLP.

Q: How abundant can glycine decarboxylase be in some plants?
A: Up to half of all mitochondrial matrix protein.

Q: What is the first product of CO2 fixation in C3 plants?
A: 3-phosphoglycerate.

Q: What is the first product of CO2 fixation in C4 plants?
A: Oxaloacetate (OAA).

Q: What enzyme in C4 mesophyll cells fixes HCO3- onto PEP?
A: PEP carboxylase.

Q: Where in C4 plants does the Calvin cycle occur?
A: In bundle sheath cells.

Q: What is the C4 carbon shuttle pathway?
A: HCO3- + PEP → oxaloacetate → malate → (transported to bundle sheath) → pyruvate + CO2 → CO2 enters Calvin cycle.

Q: What is the key feature of C4 bundle sheath cells?
A: They are gas-impermeable, allowing CO2 concentration.

Q: How does C4 RuBisCO compare to C3 RuBisCO?
A: C4 RuBisCO is faster but "sloppier"; C3 RuBisCO is slower but better at CO2 vs. O2 discrimination.

Q: What activates malate dehydrogenase in C4 plants?
A: Reduced thioredoxin.

Q: What mechanism does PEP carboxylase use?
A: A phosphorylated histidine (E-His-P) intermediate.

Q: How does pyruvate get back to PEP in C4 mesophyll cells?
A: Via pyruvate-Pi dikinase (uses ATP → AMP + PPi).

Q: Where does sucrose synthesis occur?
A: The cytosol.

Q: What activated sugar is used to make sucrose?
A: UDP-glucose.

Q: Why is UDP a good leaving group?
A: It's stabilized and released easily, driving the reaction forward.

Q: What two enzymes make sucrose from UDP-glucose + fructose-6-phosphate?
A: Sucrose-6-phosphate synthase, then sucrose-6-phosphate phosphatase.

Q: What thermodynamically drives sucrose synthesis?
A: Final Pi hydrolysis (by sucrose-6-phosphate phosphatase).

Q: What regulates sucrose synthesis?
A: Fructose-2,6-bisphosphate (F26BP), which inhibits fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase.

Q: What does high F26BP signal?
A: Dark conditions — stop making sucrose, run glycolysis instead.

Q: What activated sugar is used for starch synthesis?
A: ADP-glucose.

Q: Why is ADP a good leaving group?
A: Same reason as UDP — stable, released easily, drives reaction forward.

Q: What enzyme makes ADP-glucose from glucose-1-phosphate?
A: ADP-glucose pyrophosphorylase.

Q: What enzyme adds ADP-glucose onto a growing starch chain?
A: Starch synthase.

Q: What is the unbranched starch product called?
A: Amylose.

Q: What enzyme adds branches to starch?
A: Branching enzyme.

Q: How does starch branching compare to glycogen?
A: Starch has fewer branches than glycogen.

Q: What activates ADP-glucose pyrophosphorylase?
A: High 3-phosphoglycerate and low Pi (signals active photosynthesis).

Q: Draw: glycerate
A: HOCH2-CH(OH)-COOH (3C: hydroxymethyl – hydroxyl – carboxyl).

Q: Draw: ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate (RuBP)
A: 5C ketose: CH2OPO3 – C(=O) – CHOH – CHOH – CH2OPO3 (phosphates on C1 and C5; ketone at C2).

Q: Draw: 3-phosphoglycerate (3-PG)
A: 3C: COO- – CHOH – CH2OPO3 (carboxyl on C1, phosphate on C3).

Q: Draw: 1,3-bisphosphoglycerate (1,3-BPG)
A: 3C: phosphoanhydride at C1 (acyl phosphate) – CHOH – CH2OPO3 (phosphates on C1 and C3).

Q: Draw: glyceraldehyde 3-phosphate (G3P)
A: 3C aldose: CHO – CHOH – CH2OPO3 (aldehyde at C1, phosphate on C3).

Q: Draw: dihydroxyacetone phosphate (DHAP)
A: 3C ketose: CH2OH – C(=O) – CH2OPO3 (ketone at C2, phosphate on C3).

Q: Draw: phosphoglycolate
A: 2C: COO- – CH2OPO3 (carboxyl + phosphorylated CH2).

Q: Draw: glycolate
A: 2C: COO- – CH2OH (carboxyl + hydroxymethyl).

Q: Draw: glyoxylate
A: 2C: COO- – CHO (carboxyl + aldehyde).

Q: Draw: glycine
A: H2N-CH2-COOH (smallest amino acid).

Q: Draw: serine
A: HOCH2-CH(NH2)-COOH (amino acid with hydroxymethyl side chain).

Q: Draw: hydroxypyruvate
A: 3C: HOCH2 – C(=O) – COO- (hydroxymethyl – ketone – carboxyl).

Q: Draw: oxaloacetate (OAA)
A: HOOC-C(=O)-CH2-COOH (4C: carboxyl – ketone – CH2 – carboxyl).

Q: Draw: malate
A: HOOC-CH(OH)-CH2-COOH (4C: carboxyl – CHOH – CH2 – carboxyl).

Q: Draw: pyruvate
A: CH3-C(=O)-COO- (3C: methyl – ketone – carboxylate).

Q: Draw: UDP-glucose
A: Glucose with UDP (uridine diphosphate) attached at C1 via the anomeric oxygen.

Q: Draw: fructose 6-phosphate
A: Fructose (6C ketose, furanose form) with phosphate on C6.

Q: Draw: sucrose
A: Glucose α(1→2)β fructose disaccharide (glucose + fructose joined by an α,β-1,2 glycosidic bond).

Q: Draw: ADP-glucose
A: Glucose with ADP (adenosine diphosphate) attached at C1 via the anomeric oxygen.

Q: Draw: starch (amylose)
A: Linear chain of α-D-glucose residues linked by α(1→4) glycosidic bonds.

Q: How many steps from RuBP to 3-phosphoglycerate (carboxylase reaction)?
A: 5 steps.

Q: How many steps from 3-PG to G3P?
A: 3 steps.

Q: How many steps from RuBP to 3-PG + phosphoglycolate (oxygenase reaction)?
A: 5 steps.

Q: How many steps from phosphoglycolate to 3-PG (glycolate pathway)?
A: 8 steps.
